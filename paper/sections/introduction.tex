\section{Introduction}

The web has become the default training corpus for identity-hungry generative models, and faces are its most sensitive commodity. A handful of public portraits now suffices to synthesize a person in any context: DreamBooth-style fine-tuning binds a rare token to an identity with four to eight images~\cite{ruiz2022dreambooth}, while zero-shot adapters such as InstantID and IP-Adapter-FaceID inject a face embedding from a single reference image directly into the denoising process~\cite{wang2024instantid,ye2023ipadapter}. The consent asymmetry is severe---the subject never agreed---and it has triggered an arms race in \emph{protective perturbation}: cloaks that, added to a photo before upload, break the customization pipeline. Anti-DreamBooth established the category~\cite{le2023antidreambooth}, and a fast-growing successor literature hardens it along every laboratory axis~\cite{liu2023metacloak,zheng2023targeted,onikubo2024highfrequency,li2024promptindependent,song2024idprotector}. The narrative is reassuring: with the right perturbation, your face is yours again.

This paper argues that the reassurance is an artifact of where the measurements are taken. Every published protection is evaluated on \emph{T0-images}: clean, full-resolution, uncompressed files that the adversary kindly provides to the defense. That world does not exist on the web. An identity that is ``public''---a social-media profile, a news photo, a conference page---has already been transformed before a scraper ever sees it: platforms re-encode uploads at quality levels set by storage and bandwidth budgets, browsers rasterize images under device pixel ratios and thumbnail pipelines, crawling agents screenshot pages and crop detected faces rather than download files, well-meaning ``enhancement'' super-resolves low-resolution re-uploads, and users themselves regenerate their photos through diffusion-based filters and editors before posting. Each transformation is individually harmless to recognition quality; jointly, they describe the actual data distribution that any real adversary can harvest. We call this pipeline the \emph{web acquisition funnel}, and the resulting evaluation blind spot the \emph{Web Acquisition Gap}: the systematic difference between the protection a defense delivers on the files it was handed and the protection that survives on the files an adversary can actually obtain. The funnel, we claim, is not a nuisance variable for anti-customization protections but their primary adversary---an attack surface that requires no adversarial optimization whatsoever, costs milliseconds to seconds per image on commodity platforms, and operates \emph{before} the defense is ever consulted.

Two observations sharpen the claim. First, the funnel is not hypothetical: published measurements already show that a single JPEG re-encode at quality 85--95 neutralizes a broad class of protection perturbations~\cite{sandovalsegura2023jpeg}, that common image transformations systematically suppress unlearnable-example attacks~\cite{qin2023learning,sandovalsegura2023what}, and that robustness evaluations of protective perturbations under diffusion-aware adversaries remain unfavorable~\cite{peng2025contrastive}. Nor is the funnel's parameter range speculative: we probe production image pipelines directly and find GitHub's avatar service re-encoding at JPEG quality 75, Gravatar at 90, and WordPress's Photon CDN at 80---operating points that fall squarely inside, not at the edge of, the transcode tiers we evaluate. What is missing is a systematic, tiered, budget-aware account of the entire funnel---from platform transcode to agent capture to restoration to regeneration---and of \emph{where along the funnel} each family of defenses actually collapses, measured under both tunable and zero-shot attack pipelines. Second, the evaluation practices of the field are drifting in the opposite direction: newest defenses arrive with evaluations on ever-cleaner galleries under ever-stronger laboratory attackers~\cite{fardin2026metacloakjpeg,chen2025fragile}, and the first cross-method evaluation studies, while valuable, still hold the acquisition side fixed~\cite{wang2025privacy}. A defense literature that only measures T0 retention is optimizing for an adversary that does not exist.

We build the missing instrument. \emph{ScrapeBench} reconstructs the funnel in software: seven tiers (T0 original; T1 platform transcode; T2 browser and thumbnail rendering; T3 agent-style capture with face cropping; T4 super-resolution restoration; T5 diffusion regeneration; T6 adaptive mixing and purification), each with an explicit attacker budget. On top of it we define the \emph{retention ratio} $\mathrm{RR}(T)=\Delta(T)/\Delta(T_0)$, where $\Delta(T)$ is the protection gap (clean minus protected identity similarity under a fixed attack) at tier $T$: RR measures the fraction of a defense's headline T0 protection that survives acquisition reality. The metric is deliberately paired with a cost ledger, because the funnel's defining property is an \emph{inversion of asymmetry}---the defender pays GPU-minutes per identity to forge a perturbation, the attacker pays CPU-milliseconds per image to defeat it.

Running ScrapeBench over 40 public identities, four defenses spanning the protection design space (clean control, random noise, FSMG-style untargeted surrogate attacks~\cite{le2023antidreambooth}, and ASPL-lite alternation) plus a JPEG-aware retrained variant that stress-tests the ``just make it JPEG-robust'' fix, and three attack families (DreamBooth-LoRA fine-tuning and the zero-shot adapters IP-Adapter-FaceID and InstantID) yields four findings, each readable from the collapse curves in Section~\ref{sec:results}: \textbf{(F1)} protection sheds most of its gap within the \emph{platform-controlled} tiers (T1--T2), before any adaptive attacker enters; \textbf{(F2)} zero-shot identity adapters, which skip fine-tuning entirely, erase most of what survives the platform tiers, because the perturbations were never optimized against embedding injection; \textbf{(F3)} the cost asymmetry runs against the defender by orders of magnitude, converting ``protection'' into a tax the attacker routes around; \textbf{(F4)} the failure is structural, not parametric---reordering or composing the funnel's transformations does not restore protection, and the JPEG-aware variant that recovers part of the transcode tier stays exposed to rendering and regeneration. Two distinctions keep the interpretation honest: we separate \emph{defense bypass} (the attack succeeds on a visually intact image) from \emph{image destruction} (the tier degrades the photograph until no attack is possible), and we claim scope explicitly---four representative $T_0$-optimized defenses and two variants, bounding deployment value rather than proving impossibility. The constructive turn is a funnel-aware reporting standard (publish RR@T, not T0-only numbers) and a consent layer that survives the funnel by construction: \texttt{face-consent.txt}, a robots.txt-style consent manifest with a reference validator.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The Web Acquisition Gap and a funnel threat model.} We name and formalize the systematic gap between laboratory protection evaluation and web acquisition reality as a seven-tier funnel with per-tier attacker budgets, and anchor the tier parameters in direct measurements of production image pipelines rather than assumptions.
\item \textbf{Platform reality check.} We instrument public image services (GitHub avatars, Gravatar, WordPress Photon, an open image proxy) and show their re-encoding and resizing behavior---JPEG quality 60--90, size clamping---covers the tiers that collapse the defenses, making the threat model empirical rather than hypothetical.
\item \textbf{The retention-ratio metric and collapse-curve protocol.} $\mathrm{RR}(T)$ reduces protection robustness under acquisition to a single interpretable curve per defense; an integrated funnel score summarizes the curve; a cost ledger exposes the defender--attacker asymmetry that raw similarity numbers hide; and an infeasibility view separates defense bypass from image destruction.
\item \textbf{ScrapeBench, an open harness.} A reproducible pipeline---transcoding, browser/agent rendering, restoration, diffusion regeneration, adaptive pooling---integrated with DreamBooth-LoRA and two zero-shot attackers (IP-Adapter-FaceID, InstantID), ArcFace scoring, paired statistics, and automatic result publication.
\item \textbf{Empirical collapse findings with stress tests.} Across 40 identities, four published defenses, two controls, a JPEG-aware variant, and three attack families we quantify F1--F4, including order-and-composition ablations, establishing where---not just whether---protections fail and which repairs cannot restore them.
\item \textbf{A consent layer that survives the funnel.} We propose \texttt{face-consent.txt}, a robots.txt-style machine-readable consent manifest with a reference validator, shifting the durable line of defense from perturbations that the funnel erases to acquisition-layer provenance that the funnel carries with the image.
\end{itemize}
