\section{Introduction}

The Web's old social contract was written for human readers. A person could
publish a portrait to be seen without thereby authorizing anyone to construct a
generator of their identity. On the agentic Web, that distinction is collapsing:
crawlers can collect a few images and invoke DreamBooth~\cite{ruiz2022dreambooth},
InstantID~\cite{wang2024instantid}, or IP-Adapter-FaceID~\cite{ye2023ipadapter}
without further interaction with the subject. Pixel perturbations such as
Anti-DreamBooth, GAP-Diff, and IDProtector attempt to restore agency by making
published images unsuitable for customization~\cite{le2023antidreambooth,zhu2025gapdiff,song2024idprotector}.

\textbf{What is brave and new.} We propose that anti-customization be treated as
an \emph{acquisition contract} between people, Web infrastructure, and AI agents,
not as a property measured only on a downloaded file. The concrete contribution
is a benchmark and reference defense in which ordinary Web interventions are
first-class, independently testable contract clauses. This changes the research
question from ``does noise fool one model?'' to ``after which legitimate Web
operation does a person's refusal cease to be enforceable?'' The distinction
matters for future browsers, CDNs, agents, and policy: it makes failures
attributable and points to where consent must be preserved.

A cumulative ``seven-level funnel'' cannot answer that question. Its later
levels inherit all earlier damage, confounding the effect of a JPEG encoder with
that of a crop or diffusion reconstruction. ScrapeBench instead defines seven
\emph{single interventions}. T0 is the released image; each of T1--T6 consumes
that same T0 source independently. Clean and protected branches use identical
identities, operations, prompts, seeds, and scorers. This paired design measures
the protection gap attributable to one intervention while separately recording
whether the input or generated face remains usable.

Our current feasibility pilot fixes the first four valid identities in a
pre-audited manifest, with four gallery and two held-out reference images per
identity. They are selected before inspecting defense outcomes. An audited
40-identity pool (160 gallery images; 96.25\% face detection; .787 mean
gallery--reference ArcFace similarity) is reserved for expansion only if the
small pilot passes its attack-validity and effect-direction gates. These are
data-integrity facts, not defense results. The completed snapshot compares clean
images, GAP-Diff, MetaCloak, GoodAC, and our ARCP-Web with InstantID and
IP-Adapter-FaceID. A separate 1,000-step DreamBooth protocol is a second-stage
experiment. Released checkpoints are used where
provided; MetaCloak and GoodAC are released-code ports whose Web40 adaptations
are disclosed rather than mislabeled as official artifacts.

ARCP-Web is designed for this contract. Its matched-budget evaluation uses a
pixel-space $8/255$ cap, low/detail perturbation grids, texture-aware amplitude
allocation, and a differentiable copy of InstantID's ArcFace recognizer inside
a balanced objective spanning T0--T6. Half of its updates cover all interventions
uniformly; the rest target the intervention currently retaining the most
identity information. This is a falsifiable design, not a guarantee. We claim
an advantage at a layer only if its paired confidence interval supports one.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item an acquisition-contract formulation for face consent on the agentic Web;
  \item a non-cumulative T0--T6 benchmark with matched clean controls, explicit
  validity gates, and conditional plus end-to-end metrics;
  \item a reproducible four-identity feasibility pilot, with a preregistered
  path to a 40-identity expansion, comparing released-code robustness-oriented
  defenses and two zero-shot customization families;
  \item ARCP-Web, whose optimization allocates effort to its weakest acquisition
  intervention rather than optimizing only the pristine upload; and
  \item a machine-readable \texttt{face-consent.txt} direction for carrying
  consent when no pixel-only defense can satisfy the contract.
\end{itemize}
