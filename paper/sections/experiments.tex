\section{ScrapeBench: Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Implementation}
ScrapeBench is a single Python harness built on \texttt{diffusers}, \texttt{transformers}, \texttt{peft}, \texttt{insightface}, and Pillow. Tiers are pure image functions in a registry (\texttt{scrapebench.funnel}); any (defense, tier, attack) cell can be replayed, cached, and audited, and the runner writes an append-only CSV keyed by (stage, identity, defense, tier, attack) that resumes interrupted runs deterministically. Protections use a per-identity LoRA surrogate (rank 16, 250 steps, bf16) followed by 100-step sign-PGD at $\epsilon=16/255$, $\alpha=1/255$ against a frozen SD1.5 backbone (FSMG~\cite{le2023antidreambooth}); ASPL-lite alternates two surrogate--perturbation rounds at the same budget; the random control draws uniform $\epsilon$-ball noise; the clean control resizes and re-encodes without perturbation. Re-implementations are attributed in the repository.

\subsection{Attack Pipelines}
\emph{DreamBooth-LoRA} (Stage B) fine-tunes the SD1.5 UNet on a tiered gallery for 400 steps (rank-16 LoRA, lr $10^{-4}$, batch 4) with the standard prompt set and generates 20 images per cell (5 prompts $\times$ 4 seeds) at 30 steps, guidance 7.5. \emph{IP-Adapter-FaceID} (Stage A) loads the publicly released FaceID adapter~\cite{ye2023ipadapter} on SD1.5 at scale 0.8, embeds every gallery image through insightface/buffalo\_l and conditions generation on all reference embeddings jointly (multi-image IP-Adapter conditioning), producing 10 images per cell (5 prompts $\times$ 2 seeds) at 30 steps, guidance 5.0. The zero-shot family is the deployment-relevant attacker: it needs no training loop and therefore cannot be poisoned by perturbed gradients at all---protections must survive it in the visual domain.

\subsection{Protocol and Data}
We sample 40 identities from the CelebA face corpus~\cite{liu2015deep}, each contributing 8 gallery images and 2 held-out reference images for identity scoring. To balance compute across two GPUs, defenses are assigned to disjoint 20-identity halves (clean/random on one half, FSMG/ASPL on the other), and we additionally generate clean and random galleries on the second half so that every defense has a same-identity clean control for paired statistics; every defense is evaluated on all ten tiers of its half. Identity scoring uses the ArcFace embedding (buffalo\_l)~\cite{deng2018arcface} with $\tau=0.30$ calibrated so that unrelated-identity similarity is below $\tau$ for all clean generations. \emph{Stage A} cells: 20 identities $\times$ 4 defenses $\times$ 10 tiers (T0; T1 at JPEG $q{\in}\{95,85,75,65\}$ and WebP-80; T2 canvas DPR-1; T3 agent face-crop; T4 SR; T5 img2img-30), 800 evaluations in total, plus 60 cells for a JPEG-aware retrained variant (\emph{FSMG-J}: identical budget to FSMG, but the surrogate and PGD loops see the perturbation through a differentiable JPEG-$q$85 forward pass, testing whether robustness to the measured platform operating points can be bought at equal cost). \emph{Stage A-instant}: the strongest zero-shot attack is repeated with InstantID~\cite{wang2024instantid} as the generator on a 5-identity $\times$ 4-tier subset, testing attack-family generality. InstantID runs SDXL-base with the InstantX adapter (IP scale $0.8$) and face-keypoint ControlNet ($0.8$) at $512^{2}$, 30 steps, guidance 5.0, conditioning on the single best-detected gallery face---averaging embeddings over the multi-pose gallery collapses identity signal. Because its keypoint control degrades scene prompts to unrecognizable framings, its cells use the two portrait prompts of the shared set (ten samples per cell, identical budget). \emph{Stage O} (order and composition): 6 identities $\times$ 3 defenses $\times$ 6 transformation programs (canonical order, SR-first, regeneration-first, JPEG-only, a five-stage deep chain, and regeneration-only), testing whether reordering or composing the funnel's transformations restores what individual tiers destroy. \emph{Stage B} cells: a 12-identity subset $\times$ 3 defenses $\times$ 4 tiers (T0, T1 JPEG-85, T2, T5), each a full fine-tuning run. \emph{Stage C} cells: 12 identities spanning both halves $\times$ \{mix-20\%, mix-50\%, purify-50\} under FSMG, each a full 400-step fine-tuning run on the adapted gallery. Every number reported is the identity-averaged statistic with a 95\% bootstrap CI; paired Wilcoxon tests against the clean baseline use Holm-corrected $p$-values across tiers; Cliff's $\delta$ quantifies effect magnitude. All experiments run on two RTX 4090s; total compute is approximately 160 GPU-hours, of which the attacker side (generation) is under 35.

\subsection{What We Claim and What We Do Not}
ScrapeBench measures \emph{retention of protection under realistic acquisition}, not the strength of any particular attack: the funnel tiers are deliberately non-adversarial, chosen to mirror what platforms, browsers, agents, and users already do. Findings therefore bound the \emph{deployment} value of T0-optimized protections; they do not claim that funnel-robust protections are impossible (Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses design directions), nor that the specific defenses tested are the strongest achievable instantiations of their families. FSMG and ASPL serve as canonical representatives of the gradient-poisoning family on which the 2023--2026 defenses build; the newer generation---injection-hardening~\cite{qiu2026void}, perceptual-degradation~\cite{wang2026hierarchical,wang2025antiaesthetics}, and purification-robust~\cite{yang2025towards} designs---targets named adversaries rather than the acquisition path, and our Stage-C purification condition probes exactly the operator class those designs defend against.
