\section{ScrapeBench: Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Implementation}
ScrapeBench is a single Python harness built on \texttt{diffusers}, \texttt{transformers}, \texttt{peft}, \texttt{insightface}, and Pillow. The funnel tiers are implemented as pure image functions registered in a tier registry (\texttt{scrapebench.funnel}), so that any (defense, tier, attack) cell can be replayed, cached, and audited; the runner writes an append-only CSV keyed by (stage, identity, defense, tier, attack) and resumes interrupted runs deterministically. Protections are generated with a per-identity LoRA surrogate (rank 16) trained for 200--250 steps at bf16, followed by 100-step sign-PGD at $\epsilon=8/255$, $\alpha=1/255$ maximizing the denoising loss against a frozen SD1.5 backbone (FSMG~\cite{le2023antidreambooth}); ASPL-lite alternates two surrogate--perturbation rounds; the random-noise control draws uniform $\epsilon$-ball noise; the clean control resizes and re-encodes without perturbation. All re-implementations are attributed in the repository (AGPL-3.0 Anti-DreamBooth for the FSMG/ASPL derivations; MIT/Apache dependencies otherwise).

\subsection{Attack Pipelines}
\emph{DreamBooth-LoRA} (Stage B) fine-tunes the SD1.5 UNet on a tiered gallery for 400 steps (rank-16 LoRA, lr $10^{-4}$, batch 4) with the standard prompt set and generates 20 images per cell (5 prompts $\times$ 4 seeds) at 30 steps, guidance 7.5. \emph{IP-Adapter-FaceID} (Stage A) loads the publicly released FaceID adapter~\cite{ye2023ipadapter} on SD1.5 at scale 0.8, embeds each gallery image through insightface/buffalo\_l, averages the reference embeddings, and generates 10 images per cell (5 prompts $\times$ 2 seeds) at 25 steps, guidance 5.0. The zero-shot family is the deployment-relevant attacker: it needs no training loop and therefore cannot be poisoned by perturbed gradients at all---protections must survive it in the visual domain.

\subsection{Protocol and Data}
We sample 43 identities from a public celebrity face corpus (aligned crops~\cite{karras2017progressive}), each contributing 8 gallery images and 2 held-out reference images for identity scoring. Identity scoring uses the ArcFace embedding (buffalo\_l)~\cite{deng2018arcface} with $\tau=0.30$ calibrated so that unrelated-identity similarity is below $\tau$ for all clean generations. Stage A cells: 43 identities $\times$ 4 defenses $\times$ 10 tiers (T0; T1 at JPEG $q{\in}\{95,85,75,65\}$ and WebP-80; T2 canvas DPR-1; T3 agent face-crop; T4 SR; T5 img2img-30). Stage B cells: a 12-identity subset $\times$ 3 defenses $\times$ 4 tiers (T0, T1 JPEG-85, T2, T5), each a full fine-tuning run. Stage C cells: 12 identities $\times$ \{mix-20\%, mix-50\%, purify-50\} under FSMG. Every number reported is the identity-averaged statistic with a 95\% bootstrap CI; paired Wilcoxon tests against the clean baseline use Holm-corrected $p$-values across tiers; Cliff's $\delta$ quantifies effect magnitude. All experiments run on two RTX 4090s; total compute is approximately 150 GPU-hours, of which the attacker side (generation) is under 30.

\subsection{What We Claim and What We Do Not}
ScrapeBench measures \emph{retention of protection under realistic acquisition}, not the strength of any particular attack: the funnel tiers are deliberately non-adversarial, chosen to mirror what platforms, browsers, agents, and users already do. Findings therefore bound the \emph{deployment} value of T0-optimized protections; they do not claim that funnel-robust protections are impossible (Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses design directions), nor that the specific defenses tested are the strongest achievable instantiations of their families.
