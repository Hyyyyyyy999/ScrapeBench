\section{Results: What Survives an Honest Baseline}
\label{sec:results}

We organize the findings as F1--F4 from Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Every number is produced by the released harness from the append-only result log; retention ratios are computed against \emph{same-identity} clean baselines (the defense's own half of the identity split), because the pooled cross-half baseline inflates apparent protection by up to $3\times$ (Section~\ref{sec:res_f1}). Three planned components were excluded after integrity checks: the agent face-crop tier (T3), whose full-scale runs defeated the face detector in $25$ of $27$ cells; the Stage-C data-adaptation study, whose regenerated galleries scored implausibly low under fine-tuning; and the InstantID stress grid, interrupted before completion (a one-identity probe is reported in Section~\ref{sec:res_stress}). Appendix~\ref{app:integrity} details each exclusion.

Table~\ref{tab:stageA} is the quantitative core. Under the paired same-identity baseline the protected-state gap is real but modest: FSMG holds the zero-shot attacker at $\nAfgto$ versus $\cAfgto$ for its own clean control---a gap of $\gAfgto$ ($13\%$ relative)---and ASPL-lite at $\gAasto$ ($\nAasto$ vs.\ $\cAasto$). In the protocol used by the original papers, where the clean baseline is computed on \emph{different} identities, the same cells read as a $0.084$ gap against the pooled $\nAclto$: the cross-half baseline alone inflates apparent protection three-fold, because identity difficulty varies strongly across halves (pooled clean T0 is $\nAclto$ on one half and $0.393$ on the other). This is not an esoteric correction: every defense we evaluate reports T0 numbers against a baseline that is not identity-matched, and the practice converts a marginal effect into a headline.

The funnel then erodes what little remains, slowly and noisily. A single JPEG-95 re-encode leaves FSMG at $\rAfgja$ of its gap; JPEG-85 at $\rAfgjb$; and by the browser render at $\rAfgtb$. The intermediate tiers fluctuate around these values ($\rAfgjc$ at JPEG-75, $\rAfgjd$ at JPEG-65) because per-tier gaps of $0.02$--$0.04$ sit within one standard error of identity variance ($\pm 0.07$ per identity): no single tier separates protection from zero. The Holm-corrected paired Wilcoxon test agrees---\emph{no defense-tier pair survives correction} at any tier, including T0 ($p=\wAfgto$ for FSMG). What the funnel does reliably is remove the \emph{statistical distinguishability} that an unpaired protocol manufactures; whether the residual gap is zero or merely unmeasurable at $n{=}18$ paired identities is exactly the question RR@T is designed to keep open.

\subsection{F2: Protection Is Attack-Conditional}
\label{sec:res_f2}

The same galleries behave completely differently under the attack class they were designed for. Stage B (Table~\ref{tab:stageB}) fine-tunes DreamBooth-LoRA on the tiered galleries: FSMG's gallery holds the fine-tuning attacker at $\nBfgto$ versus $\nBclto$ for the held-out-half clean run---a $68\%$ relative reduction, with success suppressed to $\qBfgto\%$ versus $\qBclto\%$---and the effect decays along the funnel ($\nBfgjb$ at JPEG-85, $\nBfgtb$ at the browser tier, $\nBfgte$ after regeneration, versus $\nBclte$ clean). The zero-shot reader on the identical galleries, by contrast, extracts at $\nAfgto$: the defense's entire mechanism is a poisoned fine-tuning gradient, and an attacker who never fine-tunes simply does not traverse it. Protection measured against the designed attack does not predict protection against a deployment-shaped one; evaluating only the former, as the original works do, overstates the guarantee by the entire distance between $\nBfgto$ and $\nAfgto$.

\subsection{F3: Regeneration Destroys the Attack, Not the Defense}
\label{sec:res_t5}

At the expensive end of the funnel the interpretation inverts. After T5 regeneration the clean baseline itself falls to $\nAclte$ (SR $\pAclte\%$)---the attacker's own similarity collapses because regeneration degrades every photograph---while FSMG's residual cell mean drops to $\nAfgte$, yielding a retention of $\rAfgte$, \emph{above unity}. The apparent ``recovery'' of protection at T5 is image destruction wearing protection's clothes: the attacker fails not because the perturbation survived but because no usable face signal survives, which the detection-failure view (Appendix~\ref{app:detfail}) and the qualitative grid (Figure~\ref{fig:qual}) confirm. Stage O makes the point sharply: the regeneration-first program (\rOfgtf) shows the highest retention of any ordering ($1.92$), yet its clean baseline is the lowest of any program---the funnel's most expensive tier manufactures protection by damaging the asset, a trade no platform would make for every upload. Order otherwise barely matters (Table~\ref{tab:stageO}): the canonical chain retains $\rOfgca$ of the paired gap, JPEG-only $\rOfgjo$, SR-first $\rOfgsf$, the deep chain $\rOfgdc$---all within noise of one another, because there is no large gap left for ordering to destroy or restore.

\subsection{F4: Robustness Is Bought per Transformation, and Buys Little}
\label{sec:res_stress}

The stress grid (Table~\ref{tab:stress}) tests whether the two obvious repairs help. First, making the defense JPEG-aware: FSMG-J wraps its surrogate and PGD objective in a differentiable JPEG at the measured platform qualities, at the same perturbation budget. The result is null: FSMG-J holds a T0 gap of $\gAfjto$ versus FSMG's $\gAfgto$---the JPEG-aware variant is marginally \emph{weaker} at T0---and its JPEG-85 gap ($\gAfjjb$) does not separate from FSMG's ($\gAfgjb$). Robustness to the measured platform operating point bought nothing measurable, consistent with F1's finding that the platform tiers were never the dominant term. Second, a stronger reader: in a one-identity InstantID probe (SDXL, keypoint control, single-best-face conditioning; interrupted by compute limits, reported as directional evidence only), the clean baseline extracts at $0.46$ through JPEG-85 ($0.46$) and the browser tier ($0.43$) while FSMG holds at $0.36/0.33/0.23$---a gap that \emph{grows} from $0.10$ at T0 to $0.20$ at T2, opposite in sign to the standard reader's erosion. One identity cannot support a claim, but the sign flip is a warning: robustness to one zero-shot extractor does not obviously transfer to another, and RR@T curves should be reported per reader.

\subsection{The Cost Inversion}
\label{sec:res_cost}

The ledger completes the picture. Forging an FSMG perturbation costs ${\sim}500$ GPU-seconds per identity; ASPL-lite doubles it. Executing the tiers costs 5\,ms (JPEG), 40\,ms (browser render), 1.0\,s (super-resolution), and 3.5\,s (diffusion regeneration) \emph{per image} on the same hardware, so an 8-image gallery costs the attacker under $0.4$ CPU-seconds at the platform tiers---a $\kappa$ of three to four orders of magnitude in the defender's disfavor. Given F1's erosion rates the asymmetry is worse than it looks: GPU-minutes of forging buy a gap that platform milliseconds narrow to noise, and the zero-shot attacker pays nothing for a route that bypasses the mechanism entirely.

\subsection{Imperceptibility, the Binary View, and What the Attacker Sees}
\label{sec:res_qual}

The measured perturbations sit at PSNR consistent with the visibility budgets of the original works (Appendix~\ref{app:quality}). Under the binary view (Figure~\ref{fig:detfail}, Appendix~\ref{app:detfail}), the fraction of generations clearing $\tau=0.30$ tracks the same-identity gaps; at $\tau=0.25$ and $0.35$ the ordering is unchanged, indicating the findings are not artifacts of threshold choice.
