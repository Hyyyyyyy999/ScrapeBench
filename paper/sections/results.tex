\section{Results: Collapse Under the Funnel}
\label{sec:results}

We organize the findings as F1--F3 from Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Every number below is produced by the released harness; the collapse curves are plotted by \texttt{scrapebench.runner.report} from the append-only result log, and every cell links back to its inputs.

\label{sec:res_f1}

\begin{figure}[t]

Table~\ref{tab:stageA} reports mean identity similarity per defense and tier; Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} shows the retention curves and Figure~\ref{fig:gap} the absolute gaps. Two regularities stand out. First, the funnel is benign for the \emph{attack}: the clean baseline only slides from $0.358$ at T0 to $0.349$ at T2, and super-resolution (T4) actually \emph{helps} the attacker ($0.367$), confirming that tiers are not a quality knife that cuts both ways---they cut the defense. Second, protections shed their gap almost entirely within the platform's own pipeline. FSMG retains $63\%$ of its T0 gap after a single JPEG-95 re-encode, $49\%$ after JPEG-85---the default quality of several major platforms---$20\%$ after JPEG-75, and $12\%$ after JPEG-65; ASPL-lite holds up slightly longer ($75/53/35/17\%$) but follows the same trajectory. By the browser tier (T2) both defenses are at $\mathrm{RR}\approx0.1$--$0.16$, and the Holm-corrected paired Wilcoxon test against the same-half clean control, significant for every defense at T0 ($p<0.003$), becomes marginal for FSMG at JPEG-85 ($p=0.07$), is clearly gone for FSMG by JPEG-75 ($p=0.62$), and is gone for every defense by T2 ($p>0.99$): statistically, the protection has ceased to exist exactly where the web begins.

Three second-order observations sharpen the mechanism. \emph{(i) WebP-80 is gentler than JPEG-65} ($\mathrm{RR}=0.35$--$0.44$ vs. $0.05$--$0.17$): WebP's block structure re-quantizes the perturbation differently, so compression format is not interchangeable---a defense tuned against JPEG is not automatically robust. \emph{(ii) Face cropping (T3) is a cliff, not a slope}: after the agent crops the face region, FSMG's residual retention turns negative ($\mathrm{RR}=-0.11$)---the perturbation mass outside the crop was all that remained. \emph{(iii) Super-resolution partially resurrects the gap} ($\mathrm{RR}\approx0.2$--$0.3$ at T4): Real-ESRGAN restores low-frequency identity structure that survives, but it cannot restore the high-frequency gradient alignment the defense needs, so the recovery is partial and short-lived---T5 regeneration erases it again ($\mathrm{RR}\leq0.06$). The funnel is not monotone in destructive power, but every deviation from monotonicity is explained by which frequencies each tier preserves.

\subsection{F2: Zero-Shot Attacks Bypass Fine-Tuning Poisons}
\label{sec:res_f2}

Stage B (Table~\ref{tab:stageB}) measures the classic target of these defenses: DreamBooth-LoRA fine-tuning on tiered galleries. At T0 the protections deliver their advertised effect---FSMG holds the attacker at $0.236$ versus a clean baseline of $0.407$, a $42\%$ relative reduction, and the zero-shot attack of Stage A on the same galleries is \emph{weaker} ($0.307$), consistent with the defenses' design intent. The ordering reverses along the funnel. Under fine-tuning, FSMG retains $\mathrm{RR}=0.68$ at JPEG-85 and $0.48$ at T2; under the zero-shot attack on the same tiers it retains $0.49$ and $0.12$ (Figure~\ref{fig:family}). The zero-shot extractor never touches the poisoned fine-tuning gradient---it reads an identity embedding straight off the funnel output---so the defense's entire investment is evaluated against an attack that ignores it. By T5, both families converge to the same collapsed state ($|\mathrm{RR}|\leq0.16$), but they get there by different doors: the fine-tuner is defeated by gradient poisoning fading, the zero-shot adapter by the perturbation never surviving transduction at all.

\subsection{F3: Data-Level Adaptation Closes the Residual Gap}
\label{sec:res_f3}

Stage C (Table~\ref{tab:stageC}) asks whether the residual T0 protection survives an attacker who adapts at the data level, using the strongest defense (FSMG) and its strongest setting (T0). Mixing just $20\%$ clean images from a second source into the training pool restores attacker similarity from $0.236$ to $0.300$ ($\mathrm{RR}=0.63$); mixing $50\%$ restores it to $0.365$ ($\mathrm{RR}=0.25$); laundering the protected gallery through diffusion regeneration before fine-tuning (purify-50) restores it to $0.388$ ($\mathrm{RR}=0.11$), within $0.02$ of the unprotected baseline. None of these adaptations involves adversarial optimization: clean-source mixing is what a scraper naturally does when harvesting multiple sources, and purification is a single \texttt{img2img} call per image.

\subsection{The Cost Inversion}
\label{sec:res_cost}

Table~\ref{tab:cost} completes the ledger. Forging an FSMG perturbation costs $\sim$500 GPU-seconds per identity (250 surrogate-training steps plus 100 sign-PGD steps); ASPL-lite doubles it. Executing the tiers that defeat it costs $5$\,ms (JPEG transcode) to $3.5$\,s (diffusion regeneration) \emph{per image} on the same class of hardware. Per identity, an 8-image gallery at the platform tiers costs the attacker under $0.4$ CPU-seconds---a $\kappa$ of three to four orders of magnitude in the defender's disfavor, shrinking to $\sim$$20\times$ even at the most expensive tier (T5), which the attacker controls and can batch. The decisive asymmetry is not only in the ledger: the funnel's cheaper tiers are operated by platforms and users who have no stake in the attack, so the attacker pays only the attention cost of downloading.

\subsection{Imperceptibility and the Binary View}
\label{sec:res_qual}

Protection quality is only meaningful relative to image quality. The measured perturbations sit at PSNR \res{28.1}\,dB (FSMG), \res{28.4}\,dB (ASPL-lite), and \res{29.1}\,dB (random noise) against the $\epsilon=16/255$ ball---consistent with the visibility budgets of the original works. Under the binary view (Figure~\ref{fig:detfail}), the fraction of generations that clear the recognition threshold $\tau=0.30$ follows the RR collapse; at $\tau=0.25$ and $0.35$ the ordering and the tier of statistical death are unchanged, indicating the findings are not artifacts of threshold choice.
