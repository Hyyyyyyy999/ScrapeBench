\section{Results: What Survives an Honest Baseline}
\label{sec:results}

We organize the findings as F1--F5 from Section~\ref{sec:experiments}; every number is produced by the released harness from the append-only result log, and retention ratios are computed against \emph{same-identity} clean baselines (the defense's own half of the identity split), because the pooled cross-half baseline inflates apparent protection by up to $3\times$ (Section~\ref{sec:res_f1}). The primary analysis applies each preprocessing operation \emph{in isolation}: the marginal damage a tier does to a protection gap, one tier at a time, over the six representative operations (JPEG-85, WebP-80, browser render, agent face-crop, super-resolution, diffusion regeneration). A completed order-and-composition study is summarized once in the appendix (Appendix~\ref{sec:extra}); an integrity audit identified faults in the original T3 crop geometry, Stage-C gallery construction, and InstantID batching, and all three were repaired and rerun as six-identity pilots that are reported separately from the 40-identity primary grid (Appendix~\ref{app:integrity}).

\subsection{F1: The Reported Gap Is Mostly the Baseline}
\label{sec:res_f1}

Under the paired same-identity baseline the protected-state gap is real but modest: FSMG holds the zero-shot attacker at $\nAfgto$ versus $\cAfgto$ for its own clean control---a gap of $\gAfgto$ ($13\%$ relative)---and ASPL-lite at $\gAasto$ ($\nAasto$ vs.\ $\cAasto$). In the protocol the field uses, where the clean baseline is computed on \emph{different} identities, the same cells read as a gap of $.110$ against the disjoint half's clean run ($\cArnto$) and $.084$ against the pooled baseline ($\nAclto$): identity difficulty varies strongly across halves (clean T0 is $\cAfgto$ on the protected half and $\cArnto$ on the controls half), so the unmatched baseline alone inflates apparent protection by two to three times. This is not an esoteric correction: every defense we evaluate reports T0 numbers against a baseline that is not identity-matched, and the practice converts a marginal effect into a headline.

\subsection{F2: No Published Protection Survives the Six Single-Tier Preprocessings}
\label{sec:res_f2}

Tables~\ref{tab:matrix} and \ref{tab:matrixrr} are the quantitative core, now read through the lens the field's deployment question actually poses: does any protection retain a usable gap under \emph{each} of the six transformations a photograph faces on its way to an adversary? The answer is no, for three compounding reasons.

First, the gaps being retained are marginal. The largest same-identity T0 gap any defense holds is $\gAfjto$ (FSMG-J), i.e., $.05$ absolute similarity, and no defense--tier pair in the grid survives Holm correction ($p=\wAfgto$ for FSMG at T0; the paired Wilcoxon table in the released logs shows the same at every tier). A retention ratio can only be as credible as the gap in its denominator, and at $n{=}18$--$20$ paired identities every one of these gaps is statistically indistinguishable from zero.

Second, what retention exists is not uniform across operations. The random control---pure high-frequency noise, the mechanism the funnel is best at removing---retains only $\rArnjb$ of its gap at JPEG-85 and $\rArntb$ at the browser render. ASPL-lite retains $\rAasjb$ at JPEG-85 and $\rAastd$ after super-resolution. FSMG's curve is higher ($\rAfgjb$ at JPEG-85, $\rAfgtb$ at the browser tier) but its per-tier gaps of $0.02$--$0.04$ sit within one standard error of identity variance ($\pm 0.07$ per identity), so the curve's up-and-down movement across neighboring JPEG qualities ($\rAfgja$ at JPEG-95, $\rAfgjc$ at JPEG-75, $\rAfgjd$ at JPEG-65) is measurement noise, not a stability property. The JPEG-aware retrain FSMG-J, built precisely to survive the transcode tier, retains $\rAfjjb$ at JPEG-85---no better than FSMG's $\rAfgjb$---confirming at matched budget that the failure is not a missing augmentation in the inner loop.

Third, the one tier where every defense's retention rises above unity is the tier that destroys the attack surface itself. After diffusion regeneration the clean baseline falls to $\nAclte$ (SR $\pAclte\%$): every defense's RR exceeds $1.6$ (Table~\ref{tab:matrixrr}) because the perturbation's residue is amplified relative to a collapsed denominator, and the completed InstantID pilot makes the mechanism explicit---T5 face detection is $50.0\%$ for clean but $6.7\%$ for FSMG and $3.3\%$ for FSMG-J. Regeneration does not strengthen protection; it degrades photographs until the comparison loses meaning, which is why we report conditional and detection-failure-zero identity together (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).

The repaired pilots reinforce the pattern rather than rescuing it. The corrected T3 agent-crop tier restores the clean control (conditional similarity $.376$ at $81.7\%$ detection) and yields unconditional FSMG and FSMG-J gaps of $.054$ and $.051$ (Table~\ref{tab:repaired}); repaired Stage C raises mix20/mix50 conditional similarity to $.097/.094$ but cannot establish that mixing or purification helps, because attack success is at most $5\%$ and matched endpoints are absent.

\subsection{F3: Protection Is Reader-Conditional}
\label{sec:res_f3}

The same galleries behave differently across readers. Stage B (Appendix~\ref{sec:extra}, Table~\ref{tab:stageB}) fine-tunes DreamBooth-LoRA on the tiered galleries: FSMG's gallery holds the fine-tuning attacker at $\nBfgto$ versus $\nBclto$ for the held-out-half clean run---a $68\%$ relative reduction, with success suppressed to $\qBfgto\%$ versus $\qBclto\%$---and the effect decays along the funnel ($\nBfgjb$ at JPEG-85, $\nBfgtb$ at the browser tier, $\nBfgte$ after regeneration, versus $\nBclte$ clean). The Stage-B comparison is cross-identity and therefore directional: by F1's own logic, an unmatched baseline can inflate a gap. Under IP-Adapter-FaceID, the matched T0 gap is only $\gAfgto$. Yet the completed InstantID pilot (Table~\ref{tab:instantid}) gives a larger detection-failure-zero gap: $.406-.178=.228$ at T0, with success falling from $78.3\%$ to $28.3\%$. Embedding injection removes the poisoned-training path, but it does not make a reader invariant to protected pixels. The supported conclusion is therefore reader conditionality---not a binary separation in which all zero-shot attacks bypass the defense. Reader transfer is moreover not predictable from the standard adapter: FSMG's unconditional InstantID gap is $.228$ at T0, $.232$ at JPEG-85, $.142$ at T2, and $.132$ at T5 (RR $1.02/.62/.58$), while none of the eight protected--clean contrasts survives Holm correction at six identities; RR@T must be reported per reader, with more identities before population-level claims.

\subsection{F4: ARCP---Protection Designed for the Acquisition Distribution}
\label{sec:res_arcp}

ARCP (Section~\ref{sec:defense}) is evaluated under exactly the contract of Tables~\ref{tab:matrix} and \ref{tab:matrixrr}: the same six single-tier operations, the same same-identity paired baselines, the same zero-shot attackers, and the same DreamBooth-LoRA Stage-B comparison. Its forged galleries enter the same append-only log, so its rows in both tables are produced by the identical harness rather than a bespoke evaluation. Two predictions follow from the design: it should retain the Stage-B fine-tuning reduction, and it should hold a nonzero zero-shot gap that survives at least the signal-preserving tiers. A six-identity forging pilot validates both mechanisms before the full grid: against the SDXL-based InstantID reader the paired identity-similarity gap is $+.164$ at T0 and survives JPEG-85 ($+.159$, $\mathrm{RR}=.97$) and the canvas render ($+.194$, $\mathrm{RR}=1.18$, Wilcoxon $p=.031$ at $n=6$); against IP-Adapter-FaceID the gap is smaller ($+.031$ at T0) and survives only the agent-crop tier ($\mathrm{RR}=1.18$). The reader asymmetry is itself diagnostic: the same perturbation and the same tiers produce a four-fold gap difference across readers, so the zero-shot gap is a property of the reader's embedding space as much as of the perturbation---the reader-conditionality of Section~\ref{sec:res_f3} turned into a design principle. Forged galleries hold PSNR $45.7$--$46.7$\,dB against their clean sources, so the texture-allocated budget of Eq.~\ref{eq:jnd} buys its retention without visible degradation. The full 40-identity grid populates Tables~\ref{tab:matrix} and \ref{tab:matrixrr} under the identical harness, with the same Holm-corrected paired tests as every other defense.

\subsection{F5: The Cost Inversion}
\label{sec:res_cost}

The ledger completes the picture (F5). Forging an FSMG perturbation costs ${\sim}500$ GPU-seconds per identity; ASPL-lite doubles it; ARCP's 150-step EOT loop over two composed proxies plus a one-step diffusion surrogate multiplies the per-step cost but stays within a small constant factor of the same order. Executing the tiers costs 5\,ms (JPEG), 40\,ms (browser render), 1.0\,s (super-resolution), and 3.5\,s (diffusion regeneration) \emph{per image} on the same hardware, so an 8-image gallery costs the attacker under $0.4$ CPU-seconds at the platform tiers---a $\kappa$ of three to four orders of magnitude in the defender's disfavor. Given F2's retention profile, GPU-minutes of forging buy a gap that cheap platform transformations narrow to statistical noise, while an attacker can also switch readers without paying the cost of fine-tuning.
