\section{Results: Collapse Under the Funnel}
\label{sec:results}

We organize the findings as F1--F3 from Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Every number below is produced by the released harness; the collapse curves are plotted by \texttt{scrapebench.runner.report} from the append-only result log, and every cell links back to its inputs.

\subsection{F1: Protection Collapses at the Platform Tiers}
\label{sec:res_f1}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering

\caption{Collapse curves under the Stage-A zero-shot attack (IP-Adapter-FaceID): retention ratio $\mathrm{RR}(T)=\Delta(T)/\Delta(T_0)$ by funnel tier. Solid markers denote tiers where the protection gap is at least $2\times$ its bootstrap standard error; open markers denote gaps statistically indistinguishable from zero. Random-noise is omitted because its T0 gap does not clear this bar.}
\label{fig:collapse}

Table~\ref{tab:stageA} reports the full Stage-A grid---similarity and success rate for every (defense, tier) cell---and is the quantitative core of the paper; Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} shows the retention curves. Two regularities stand out. First, the funnel is benign for the \emph{attack}: the clean baseline only slides from $0.358$ at T0 ($\mathrm{SR}=91\%$) to $0.349$ at T2 ($\mathrm{SR}=85\%$), and super-resolution actually \emph{helps} the attacker ($0.367$, $\mathrm{SR}=93\%$)---tiers are not a quality knife that cuts both ways; they cut the defense. Second, protections shed their gap within the platform's own pipeline. FSMG retains $63\%$ of its T0 gap after a single JPEG-95 re-encode, $49\%$ after JPEG-85---the default quality of several major platforms---$20\%$ after JPEG-75, and $12\%$ after JPEG-65; ASPL-lite holds up slightly longer ($75/53/35/17\%$). In success-rate terms, ASPL suppresses the attack to $37.5\%$ at T0, one JPEG-95 pass lifts it to $52\%$, and by the browser tier every defense sits within $4$ points of the clean baseline's $85\%$. The Holm-corrected paired Wilcoxon test against the same-half clean control, significant for every defense at T0 ($p<0.003$), is marginal for FSMG at JPEG-85 ($p=0.07$), gone for FSMG by JPEG-75 ($p=0.62$), and gone for every defense by T2 ($p>0.99$): statistically, the protection ceases to exist exactly where the web begins.

Three second-order observations sharpen the mechanism. \emph{(i) WebP-80 is gentler than JPEG-65} ($\mathrm{RR}=0.35$--$0.44$ vs. $0.05$--$0.17$): compression format is not interchangeable, so a defense tuned against JPEG is not automatically robust. \emph{(ii) Face cropping (T3) is a cliff, not a slope}: after the agent crops the face region, FSMG's residual retention turns negative ($\mathrm{RR}=-0.11$)---the perturbation mass outside the crop was all that remained. \emph{(iii) Super-resolution partially resurrects the gap} ($\mathrm{RR}\approx0.2$--$0.3$ at T4): restoration recovers low-frequency identity structure but not the high-frequency gradient alignment the defense needs, and T5 regeneration erases the recovery ($\mathrm{RR}\leq0.06$). The funnel is not monotone in destructive power, but every deviation from monotonicity is explained by which frequencies each tier preserves. Summarizing each curve by its funnel-integrated protection $\overline{\mathrm{RR}}$ (bottom block of Table~\ref{tab:stageA}): ASPL retains $0.293$ $[0.240,0.356]$ of its T0 gap on average across the nine post-T0 tiers, FSMG $0.215$ $[0.137,0.304]$, random noise $0.140$ $[0.028,0.285]$---a ranking that survives uncertainty, with absolute values far below the unit retention a deployer would need.

\subsection{F2: Zero-Shot Attacks Bypass Fine-Tuning Poisons}
\label{sec:res_f2}

Stage B (Table~\ref{tab:stageB}) measures the classic target of these defenses: DreamBooth-LoRA fine-tuning on tiered galleries. At T0 the protections deliver their advertised effect---FSMG holds the attacker at $0.236$ versus a clean $0.407$ (a $42\%$ relative reduction) and in binary terms holds attack success at $12.1\%$ versus $97.5\%$---while the zero-shot attack of Stage A on the same galleries is \emph{weaker} ($0.307$), consistent with design intent. The ordering reverses along the funnel: under fine-tuning FSMG retains $\mathrm{RR}=0.68$ at JPEG-85 and $0.48$ at T2, but under the zero-shot attack on the same tiers it retains $0.49$ and $0.12$ . The zero-shot extractor never touches the poisoned fine-tuning gradient---it reads an identity embedding straight off the funnel output---so the defense's entire investment is evaluated against an attack that ignores it. By T5 both families converge to the same collapsed state ($|\mathrm{RR}|\leq0.16$), by different doors: the fine-tuner is defeated by gradient poisoning fading, the zero-shot adapter by the perturbation never surviving transduction at all.

\subsection{F3: Data-Level Adaptation Closes the Residual Gap}
\label{sec:res_f3}

Stage C asks whether the residual T0 protection survives an attacker who adapts at the data level, using FSMG at T0 (reference $0.236$/$\mathrm{SR}\,12.1\%$ versus clean $0.407$/$97.5\%$). Mixing $20\%$ clean images from a second source into the training pool restores attacker similarity to $0.300$ ($\mathrm{RR}=0.63$, $\mathrm{SR}=49.2\%$); mixing $50\%$ restores it to $0.365$ ($\mathrm{RR}=0.25$, $\mathrm{SR}=87.9\%$, indistinguishable from clean); laundering the gallery through diffusion regeneration before fine-tuning restores it to $0.388$ ($\mathrm{RR}=0.11$, $\mathrm{SR}=92.1\%$), within $0.02$ of the unprotected baseline. None of these adaptations involves adversarial optimization: clean-source mixing is what a scraper naturally does when harvesting multiple sources, and purification is a single \texttt{img2img} call per image---precisely the operator class that purification-robust defenses anticipate~\cite{yang2025towards}, which is why Stage C treats it as a first-class condition.

\subsection{The Cost Inversion}
\label{sec:res_cost}

The ledger completes the picture. Forging an FSMG perturbation costs ${\sim}500$ GPU-seconds per identity; ASPL-lite doubles it. Executing the tiers that defeat it costs 5\,ms (JPEG), 40\,ms (browser render), 1.0\,s (super-resolution), and 3.5\,s (diffusion regeneration) \emph{per image} on the same hardware, so an 8-image gallery costs the attacker under $0.4$ CPU-seconds at the platform tiers---a $\kappa$ of three to four orders of magnitude in the defender's disfavor, shrinking to ${\sim}20\times$ even at T5, which the attacker controls and can batch. The asymmetry is not only economic: the cheaper tiers are operated by platforms and users who have no stake in the attack.

\subsection{Imperceptibility, the Binary View, and What the Attacker Sees}
\label{sec:res_qual}

Protection quality is only meaningful relative to image quality. The measured perturbations sit at PSNR \res{28.1}\,dB (FSMG), \res{28.4}\,dB (ASPL-lite), and \res{29.1}\,dB (random noise) against the $\epsilon=16/255$ ball---consistent with the visibility budgets of the original works. Under the binary view (Figure~\ref{fig:detfail}, Appendix~\ref{app:detfail}), the fraction of generations that clear the recognition threshold $\tau=0.30$ follows the RR collapse; at $\tau=0.25$ and $0.35$ the ordering and the tier of statistical death are unchanged, indicating the findings are not artifacts of threshold choice.

Figure~\ref{fig:qual} grounds these statistics in what the attacker's pipeline actually consumes. Platform tiers leave both galleries visually intact---which is exactly why they are dangerous: the funnel damages the \emph{perturbation} without damaging the \emph{photograph}. The costly tiers invert the picture: after T5 regeneration at strength $0.3$, the clean identity still yields recognizable faces, while the FSMG-protected gallery is degraded past the point where the attack stack's face detector finds a face at all. This is protection by destruction, not by deception: the defender wins the cell only by destroying the asset's value---and a host that regenerated every upload this way would destroy face memorability for \emph{everyone}, protected or not.

\subsection{F4: Order and Composition of the Funnel}
\label{sec:res_o}

Stage O asks whether the collapse depends on the canonical order of the funnel: an attacker who applies the same primitives in a different sequence, or only a subset, may pay less for the same effect. Table~\ref{tab:stageO} reports the retention of the FSMG gap (normalized by the paired Stage-A T0 gap on the same six identities) under six programs. JPEG-85 alone (\rOfgjo) already removes most of the protection, matching the T1 evidence; regeneration alone (\rOfgtvo) is nearly as destructive while skipping every platform tier; and the full deep chain (\rOfgdc) is not consistently worse than its cheapest ingredient---the funnel's damage is concentrated in its lossy primitives, not their length. Reordering matters only at the margins: restoring before transcoding (\rOfgsf) preserves slightly more of the gap than the canonical order, because super-resolution amplifies the perturbation before the transcode quantizes it away, whereas regenerating first (\rOfgtf) collapses as deeply as the full chain. For the defender the implication is uncomfortable: no ordering of the funnel lets a protection survive.

\subsection{F5: Stress Tests: Stronger Attack, JPEG-Aware Defense}
\label{sec:res_stress}

Two stress tests probe the boundary of the findings (Table~\ref{tab:stress}). First, replacing IP-Adapter-FaceID with InstantID (SDXL, ControlNet-guided injection), the strongest open zero-shot extractor, on a 6-identity, 4-tier subset: the InstantID baseline (\nInclto{} at T0) extracts identity as effectively as the smaller model, and protections collapse along the same trajectory---FSMG holds \nInfgto{} at T0 and \nInfgjb{} after one JPEG-85 pass. Second, FSMG-J, whose surrogate and PGD objective are wrapped in a differentiable JPEG at the measured platform qualities, is evaluated under the standard attack. At T0 it protects comparably to FSMG (\nIffjto{} vs.\ \nIffgto{}), and its advantage shows exactly where it was trained: at JPEG-85 it retains \nIffjjb{} versus FSMG's \nIffgjb{}---the only setting where a defense keeps most of its gap after a platform transcode. By T2 the advantage is gone and at T5 both collapse: robustness can be bought for one transformation class at one quality, but the funnel always contains a tier the defense was not built for.
