\section{Results: What Survives an Honest Baseline}
\label{sec:results}

We organize the findings as F1--F5 from Section~\ref{sec:experiments}; every number is produced by the released harness from the append-only result log, and retention ratios are computed against \emph{same-identity} clean baselines (the defense's own half of the identity split), because the pooled cross-half baseline inflates apparent protection by up to $3\times$ (Section~\ref{sec:res_f1}). The primary analysis applies each preprocessing operation \emph{in isolation}---the marginal damage a tier does to a protection gap, one tier at a time, over the six representative operations. An integrity audit identified faults in the original T3 crop geometry, Stage-C gallery construction, and InstantID batching; all three were repaired and rerun as six-identity pilots reported separately from the 40-identity primary grid (Appendix~\ref{app:integrity}).

\subsection{F1: The Reported Gap Is Mostly the Baseline}
\label{sec:res_f1}

Under the paired same-identity baseline the protected-state gap is real but modest: FSMG holds the zero-shot attacker at $\nAfgto$ versus $\cAfgto$ for its own clean control---a gap of $\gAfgto$ ($13\%$ relative)---and ASPL-lite at $\gAasto$ ($\nAasto$ vs.\ $\cAasto$). In the protocol the field uses, where the clean baseline is computed on \emph{different} identities, the same cells read as a gap of $.124$ against the disjoint half's clean run ($\cArnto$) and $.084$ against the pooled baseline ($\nAclto$): identity difficulty varies strongly across halves (clean T0 is $\cAfgto$ on the protected half and $\cArnto$ on the controls half), so the unmatched baseline alone inflates apparent protection by two to three times---and the practice converts a marginal effect into a headline.

\subsection{F2: No Published Protection Survives the Six Single-Tier Preprocessings}
\label{sec:res_f2}

Tables~\ref{tab:matrix} and~\ref{tab:matrixrr} are the quantitative core, read through the deployment question: does any protection retain a usable gap under \emph{each} of the six transformations a photograph faces on its way to an adversary? The answer is no, for three compounding reasons.

First, the gaps being retained are marginal. The largest same-identity T0 gap any defense holds is $\gAfjto$ (FSMG-J), $.05$ absolute similarity, and no defense--tier pair at the five signal-preserving operations survives Holm correction ($p=\wAfgto$ for FSMG at T0; the released Wilcoxon table shows the same at every signal-preserving tier). A retention ratio is only as credible as the gap in its denominator, and at the grid's paired sample sizes ($18$ identities for FSMG and ASPL-lite, $21$--$22$ for random, $6$ for FSMG-J) every one of these gaps is statistically indistinguishable from zero.

Second, what retention exists is not uniform across operations. The random control---pure high-frequency noise---holds the smallest baseline gap ($\gArnto$ at T0) and retains $\rArnjb$ of it at JPEG-85 and $\rArntb$ at the browser render; its above-unity ratio after regeneration ($\rArnte$) is the collapsed-denominator artifact quantified below, not robustness. ASPL-lite retains $\rAasjb$ at JPEG-85 and $\rAastd$ after super-resolution. FSMG's curve is higher ($\rAfgjb$ at JPEG-85, $\rAfgtb$ at the browser tier), but its per-tier gaps of $0.03$--$0.05$ sit within one standard error of identity variance ($\pm 0.07$), so its movement across neighboring JPEG qualities ($\rAfgja/\rAfgjc/\rAfgjd$ at JPEG-95/75/65) is measurement noise, not stability. The JPEG-aware retrain FSMG-J, built precisely to survive the transcode tier, retains $\rAfjjb$ at JPEG-85---no better than FSMG's $\rAfgjb$---confirming at matched budget that the failure is not a missing augmentation in the inner loop.

Third, the one tier where every audited defense's retention rises above unity is the tier that destroys the attack surface itself. After diffusion regeneration the clean baseline falls to $\nAclte$ (SR $\pAclte\%$): every audited defense's RR exceeds $1.3$ (FSMG $\rAfgte$, ASPL-lite $\rAaste$, FSMG-J $\rAfjte$, random $\rArnte$; Table~\ref{tab:matrixrr}) because the perturbation's residue is amplified relative to a collapsed denominator---FSMG and random do retain Holm-corrected gaps here ($p=\wAfgte$ and $\wArnte$), but against a destroyed baseline, not retained protection---and the InstantID pilot makes the mechanism explicit: T5 detection is $50.0\%$ for clean but $6.7\%$ for FSMG and $3.3\%$ for FSMG-J. Regeneration does not strengthen protection; it degrades photographs until the comparison loses meaning (hence the conditional and detection-failure-zero views of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).

The repaired pilots reinforce the pattern rather than rescuing it: the corrected T3 agent-crop tier restores the clean control (conditional similarity $.376$ at $81.7\%$ detection) and yields unconditional FSMG and FSMG-J gaps of $.054$ and $.051$ (Table~\ref{tab:repaired}), while repaired Stage C raises mix20/mix50 conditional similarity to $.097/.094$ but cannot establish that mixing or purification helps, because attack success is at most $5\%$ and matched endpoints are absent.

\subsection{F3: Protection Is Reader-Conditional}
\label{sec:res_f3}

The same galleries behave differently across readers. Stage B (Appendix~\ref{sec:extra}, Table~\ref{tab:stageB}) fine-tunes DreamBooth-LoRA on the tiered galleries: FSMG's gallery holds the fine-tuning attacker at $\nBfgto$ versus $\nBclto$ for the held-out-half clean run---a $68\%$ relative reduction, with success suppressed to $\qBfgto\%$ versus $\qBclto\%$---and the effect decays along the funnel ($\nBfgjb$ at JPEG-85, $\nBfgtb$ at the browser tier, $\nBfgte$ after regeneration, versus $\nBclte$ clean). The Stage-B comparison is cross-identity and therefore directional: by F1's own logic, an unmatched baseline can inflate a gap. Under IP-Adapter-FaceID, the matched T0 gap is only $\gAfgto$. Yet the completed InstantID pilot (Table~\ref{tab:instantid}) gives a larger detection-failure-zero gap: $.406-.178=.228$ at T0, with success falling from $78.3\%$ to $28.3\%$. Embedding injection removes the poisoned-training path but does not make a reader invariant to protected pixels---reader conditionality, not a binary separation in which all zero-shot attacks bypass the defense. Reader transfer is moreover not predictable from the standard adapter: FSMG's unconditional InstantID gap is $.228$ at T0, $.232$ at JPEG-85, $.142$ at T2, and $.132$ at T5 (RR $1.02/.62/.58$), and none of the eight protected--clean contrasts survives Holm correction at six identities; RR@T must be reported per reader, with more identities before population-level claims.

\subsection{F4: ARCP---Protection Designed for the Acquisition Distribution}
\label{sec:res_arcp}

ARCP (Section~\ref{sec:defense}) is evaluated under exactly the contract of Tables~\ref{tab:matrix} and \ref{tab:matrixrr}: the same six single-tier operations, same-identity paired baselines, zero-shot attackers, and DreamBooth-LoRA Stage-B comparison, with forged galleries entering the same append-only log so that its rows are produced by the identical harness rather than a bespoke evaluation. A six-identity forging pilot validates the mechanisms on the reader the design targets: against the SDXL-based InstantID reader the paired identity-similarity gap is $+.164$ at T0 (Wilcoxon $p=.094$) and survives JPEG-85 ($+.159$, $\mathrm{RR}=.97$, $p=.062$) and the canvas render ($+.194$, $\mathrm{RR}=1.18$, $p=.031$ at $n=6$), while the regeneration tier retains less ($+.060$, $\mathrm{RR}=.37$, $p=.438$); against IP-Adapter-FaceID the same galleries produce no protection (paired gap $+.031$ at T0, $p=.31$; point estimates between $-.025$ and $+.036$ across the other tiers, all $p\ge.31$). The same perturbation and tiers produce a five-fold gap difference across readers---reader conditionality turned into a design principle, and into its limit case. On IP-Adapter-FaceID, at the pilot weight $\lambda=.5$ the paired gap is statistically null at every tier, and on the 20-identity half-grid, raising the reader-term weight to $\lambda=2$ turns the gap consistently negative---T0 itself at $-.011$, JPEG-85 $-.034$ ($p=.043$), WebP-80 $-.029$ ($p=.099$), canvas $-.030$ ($p=.060$), super-resolution $-.030$ ($p=.024$), regeneration $-.016$ ($p=.229$), all uncorrected at $n=18$---pushing attacked generations toward the protected identity once the repulsion term outweighs the perceptual budget; no ARCP contrast on this reader survives Holm correction, consistent with F1's diagnosis rather than contradicting it. The texture-allocated budget of Eq.~\ref{eq:jnd} caps the perturbation amplitude at $\epsilon=16/255$ in the released implementation; per-image PSNR for the frozen ARCP galleries was not logged (the audited defenses measure $28.1$--$29.1$\,dB in the preview run), so we claim no visibility figure here.

\subsection{F5: The Cost Inversion}
\label{sec:res_cost}

The ledger completes the picture (F5): forging an FSMG perturbation costs ${\sim}500$ GPU-seconds per identity, ASPL-lite doubles it, and ARCP's 150-step EOT loop over two composed proxies plus a one-step diffusion surrogate stays within a small constant factor of the same order. Executing the tiers costs 5\,ms (JPEG), 40\,ms (browser render), 1.0\,s (super-resolution), and 3.5\,s (diffusion regeneration) \emph{per image} on the same hardware, so an 8-image gallery costs the attacker under $0.4$ CPU-seconds at the platform tiers---a $\kappa$ of three to four orders of magnitude in the defender's disfavor. Given F2's retention profile, GPU-minutes of forging buy a gap that platform milliseconds narrow to statistical noise---and the attacker can switch readers for free.
