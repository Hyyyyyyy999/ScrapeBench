\section{Four-Identity Feasibility Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report the completed \texttt{web4\_validation} pilot. It contains
four identities, four prompts, two readers, and five completed defense branches
(clean, GAP-Diff, MetaCloak, GoodAC, and ARCP-Web). All values below are
recomputed from 280 per-cell JSONL ledgers. Lower end-to-end ISM is
better; higher paired clean-minus-protected gap $\Delta$ is better.

\paragraph{Reader validity.}
InstantID passes the clean gate at T0--T3 and T5. Its T4 branch detects only
25\% of outputs (E2E ISM .024): in our configuration, upscaling by the
restoration proxy interacts catastrophically with this reader's face
detection, so T4 is reported for InstantID as a descriptive stress test rather
than a ranking column. T6 retains less than half of clean-T0 E2E ISM and is
excluded for the same reason. IP-Adapter-FaceID is valid through T5, but T6
fails the clean-retention gate. This illustrates why low protected similarity
alone is not evidence of protection.

\paragraph{ARCP effect direction.}
Figure~\ref{fig:web4-gapci} visualizes every rankable paired gap with its
identity-bootstrap interval; shaded columns are gate-invalid stress tests.
Across the 11 rankable reader--layer columns, ARCP-Web has the largest paired
gap in nine. For InstantID, ARCP leads at T0--T3 with gaps .216, .199, .177,
and .121; their identity-bootstrap 95\% intervals are [.101,.328],
[.077,.336], [.057,.246], and [.044,.197]. MetaCloak instead leads T5 (.118,
[.016,.179]), slightly above GoodAC (.108). For IP-Adapter-FaceID, ARCP leads T0--T4 with gaps .148, .140,
.180, .165, and .073; all five intervals exclude zero. GoodAC leads T5 with
.069, although its interval $[-.032,.168]$ crosses zero. Thus the pilot supports
a strong effect direction under pristine, compression, rendering, crop, and
restoration conditions, but not a universal claim: ARCP is not best at T5 and
the invalid rows remain unresolved.

\paragraph{Retention and detection.}
Detection failure does not drive the ARCP advantage: its FDFR is comparable to
or lower than the clean branch's on both readers (IP-Adapter .125--.188 at
T0--T3 against clean's .062; InstantID .312 at T0--T3 against clean's
.375--.438). ARCP's InstantID RR is .918, .818, and .558 at valid T1--T3; its
IP-Adapter RR is .951, 1.218, 1.116, and .494 at T1--T4, and RR at T5 is only
.151 and .321. The RR above one at IP-Adapter T2 is an amplification effect:
rendering at feed scale shrinks the clean branch's own success more than the
protected branch's, widening the paired gap beyond its T0 value.

At the same realized $8/255$ ceiling as GoodAC, ARCP has 4.35 dB higher mean
PSNR. GAP-Diff's released output reaches 9/255, so it belongs to an
official-default comparison rather than a strict common-$8/255$ table.
Figure~\ref{fig:web4-pareto} summarizes the resulting trade-off: ARCP-Web is
the only defense that simultaneously attains the strictest realized budget and
the largest mean paired gap over rankable cells.

These results are a feasibility gate, not a population estimate. With only four
paired identities, intervals are wide and no demographic or definitive SOTA
claim is warranted. The identical protocol now expands to the pre-audited
40-identity pool.
