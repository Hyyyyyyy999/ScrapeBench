\section{Results: Collapse Under the Funnel}
\label{sec:results}

We organize the findings as F1--F3 from Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Every number below is produced by the released harness; the collapse curves are plotted by \texttt{scrapebench.runner.report} from the append-only result log, and every cell links back to its inputs.

\subsection{F1: Protection Collapses at the Platform Tiers}
\label{sec:res_f1}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\columnwidth]{../figures/collapse_stage_A.png}
\caption{Collapse curves under the Stage-A zero-shot attack (IP-Adapter-FaceID): retention ratio $\mathrm{RR}(T)=\Delta(T)/\Delta(T_0)$ by funnel tier. Lower is worse; the dashed line marks full retention. Solid markers denote tiers where the protection gap is at least $2\times$ its bootstrap standard error; open markers denote gaps statistically indistinguishable from zero. Random-noise is omitted because its T0 gap does not clear this bar.}
\label{fig:collapse}
\end{figure}

Table~\ref{tab:stageA} reports the full Stage-A grid---mean identity similarity and binary success rate for every (defense, tier) cell---and is the quantitative core of the paper; Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} shows the retention curves. Two regularities stand out. First, the funnel is benign for the \emph{attack}: the clean baseline only slides from $0.358$ at T0 ($\mathrm{SR}=91\%$) to $0.349$ at T2 ($\mathrm{SR}=85\%$), and super-resolution (T4) actually \emph{helps} the attacker ($0.367$, $\mathrm{SR}=93\%$), confirming that tiers are not a quality knife that cuts both ways---they cut the defense. Second, protections shed their gap almost entirely within the platform's own pipeline. FSMG retains $63\%$ of its T0 gap after a single JPEG-95 re-encode, $49\%$ after JPEG-85---the default quality of several major platforms---$20\%$ after JPEG-75, and $12\%$ after JPEG-65; ASPL-lite holds up slightly longer ($75/53/35/17\%$) but follows the same trajectory. In success-rate terms, ASPL suppresses the attack to $37.5\%$ at T0, but a single JPEG-95 re-encode already lifts it to $52\%$, and by the browser tier every defense sits within $4$ points of the clean baseline's $85\%$. The Holm-corrected paired Wilcoxon test against the same-half clean control, significant for every defense at T0 ($p<0.003$), becomes marginal for FSMG at JPEG-85 ($p=0.07$), is clearly gone for FSMG by JPEG-75 ($p=0.62$), and is gone for every defense by T2 ($p>0.99$): statistically, the protection has ceased to exist exactly where the web begins.

Three second-order observations sharpen the mechanism. \emph{(i) WebP-80 is gentler than JPEG-65} ($\mathrm{RR}=0.35$--$0.44$ vs. $0.05$--$0.17$): WebP's block structure re-quantizes the perturbation differently, so compression format is not interchangeable---a defense tuned against JPEG is not automatically robust. \emph{(ii) Face cropping (T3) is a cliff, not a slope}: after the agent crops the face region, FSMG's residual retention turns negative ($\mathrm{RR}=-0.11$)---the perturbation mass outside the crop was all that remained. \emph{(iii) Super-resolution partially resurrects the gap} ($\mathrm{RR}\approx0.2$--$0.3$ at T4): Real-ESRGAN restores low-frequency identity structure that survives, but it cannot restore the high-frequency gradient alignment the defense needs, so the recovery is partial and short-lived---T5 regeneration erases it again ($\mathrm{RR}\leq0.06$). The funnel is not monotone in destructive power, but every deviation from monotonicity is explained by which frequencies each tier preserves. Summarizing each curve by its funnel-integrated protection $\overline{\mathrm{RR}}$ (bottom block of Table~\ref{tab:stageA}), ASPL retains $0.293$ $[0.240,0.356]$ of its T0 gap on average across the nine post-T0 tiers, FSMG $0.215$ $[0.137,0.304]$, and random noise $0.140$ $[0.028,0.285]$---a ranking that survives uncertainty, but whose absolute values all sit far below the unit retention a deployer would need.

\subsection{F2: Zero-Shot Attacks Bypass Fine-Tuning Poisons}
\label{sec:res_f2}

Stage B (Table~\ref{tab:stageB}) measures the classic target of these defenses: DreamBooth-LoRA fine-tuning on tiered galleries. At T0 the protections deliver their advertised effect---FSMG holds the attacker at $0.236$ versus a clean baseline of $0.407$, a $42\%$ relative reduction, and in binary terms holds attack success at $12.1\%$ versus $97.5\%$ for the unprotected gallery---while the zero-shot attack of Stage A on the same galleries is \emph{weaker} ($0.307$), consistent with the defenses' design intent. The ordering reverses along the funnel. Under fine-tuning, FSMG retains $\mathrm{RR}=0.68$ at JPEG-85 and $0.48$ at T2; under the zero-shot attack on the same tiers it retains $0.49$ and $0.12$ (Figure~\ref{fig:family}). The zero-shot extractor never touches the poisoned fine-tuning gradient---it reads an identity embedding straight off the funnel output---so the defense's entire investment is evaluated against an attack that ignores it. By T5, both families converge to the same collapsed state ($|\mathrm{RR}|\leq0.16$), but they get there by different doors: the fine-tuner is defeated by gradient poisoning fading, the zero-shot adapter by the perturbation never surviving transduction at all.

\subsection{F3: Data-Level Adaptation Closes the Residual Gap}
\label{sec:res_f3}

Stage C (Table~\ref{tab:stageC}) asks whether the residual T0 protection survives an attacker who adapts at the data level, using the strongest defense (FSMG) and its strongest setting (T0). Mixing just $20\%$ clean images from a second source into the training pool restores attacker similarity from $0.236$ to $0.300$ ($\mathrm{RR}=0.63$) and success rate from $12.1\%$ to $49.2\%$; mixing $50\%$ restores it to $0.365$ ($\mathrm{RR}=0.25$, $\mathrm{SR}=87.9\%$, statistically indistinguishable from the clean baseline); laundering the protected gallery through diffusion regeneration before fine-tuning (purify-50) restores it to $0.388$ ($\mathrm{RR}=0.11$, $\mathrm{SR}=92.1\%$), within $0.02$ of the unprotected baseline. None of these adaptations involves adversarial optimization: clean-source mixing is what a scraper naturally does when harvesting multiple sources, and purification is a single \texttt{img2img} call per image---precisely the operator class that purification-robust defenses anticipate~\cite{yang2025towards}, which is why Stage C includes it as a first-class condition rather than an afterthought.

\subsection{The Cost Inversion}
\label{sec:res_cost}

Table~\ref{tab:cost} completes the ledger. Forging an FSMG perturbation costs $\sim$500 GPU-seconds per identity (250 surrogate-training steps plus 100 sign-PGD steps); ASPL-lite doubles it. Executing the tiers that defeat it costs $5$\,ms (JPEG transcode) to $3.5$\,s (diffusion regeneration) \emph{per image} on the same class of hardware. Per identity, an 8-image gallery at the platform tiers costs the attacker under $0.4$ CPU-seconds---a $\kappa$ of three to four orders of magnitude in the defender's disfavor, shrinking to $\sim$$20\times$ even at the most expensive tier (T5), which the attacker controls and can batch. The decisive asymmetry is not only in the ledger: the funnel's cheaper tiers are operated by platforms and users who have no stake in the attack, so the attacker pays only the attention cost of downloading.

\subsection{Imperceptibility, the Binary View, and What the Attacker Sees}
\label{sec:res_qual}

Protection quality is only meaningful relative to image quality. The measured perturbations sit at PSNR \res{28.1}\,dB (FSMG), \res{28.4}\,dB (ASPL-lite), and \res{29.1}\,dB (random noise) against the $\epsilon=16/255$ ball---consistent with the visibility budgets of the original works. Under the binary view (Figure~\ref{fig:detfail}), the fraction of generations that clear the recognition threshold $\tau=0.30$ follows the RR collapse; at $\tau=0.25$ and $0.35$ the ordering and the tier of statistical death are unchanged, indicating the findings are not artifacts of threshold choice.

Platform tiers leave both galleries visually intact; the adversarially expensive tiers do the defenses' work for them---T5 regeneration at strength $0.3$ leaves the clean identity recognizable while the FSMG-protected gallery is degraded past face detectability, at which point the attack is not merely inaccurate but infeasible.}

Figure~\ref{fig:qual} grounds these statistics in what the attacker's pipeline actually consumes. The platform tiers (T1--T2) leave both the clean and the FSMG-protected gallery visually intact---which is exactly why they are dangerous: the funnel damages the \emph{perturbation} without damaging the \emph{photograph}. The costly tiers invert the picture: after T5 regeneration at strength $0.3$, the clean identity still yields recognizable faces, while the FSMG-protected gallery is degraded past the point where the face detector inside the attack stack finds a face at all. This is protection by destruction, not by deception: the defender wins the cell, but by destroying the asset's value rather than by steering the attacker's model, and the honest framing matters for deployers---a social-media host that regenerates uploads at strength $0.3$ would destroy face memorability for \emph{everyone}, protected or not.
