A rapidly growing family of ``protective perturbation'' tools promises to make personal photos unlearnable by unauthorized text-to-image customization, and deployment of such tools has begun. Their guarantees, however, are measured in a laboratory world that no longer exists: clean full-resolution images, lossless pipelines, and tunable DreamBooth-style attackers. In practice, the images an adversary trains on are \emph{scraped}, and every scraped image has already passed through a lossy acquisition funnel---platform transcoding, browser and thumbnail rendering, agent-side capture, super-resolution ``restoration,'' and diffusion-based regeneration. We present \emph{ScrapeBench}, the first evaluation harness that treats this acquisition funnel as a first-class attack surface against anti-customization protections. ScrapeBench formalizes the funnel as seven tiers (T0--T6) with explicit per-image attacker budgets, and introduces the \emph{retention ratio} RR$(T)$, which measures how much of a defense's clean-domain protection survives at funnel tier $T$. Across 40 identities from a public celebrity face corpus, two published defenses and two controls, and two attack families (fine-tuning-based DreamBooth-LoRA and zero-shot IP-Adapter-FaceID), the collapse is fast and one-sided: protections that are strong at T0 lose most of their protection gap by the platform's own compression and rendering tiers (T1--T2), before any adaptive attacker enters; zero-shot identity injection, which never touches the fine-tuning gradients the defenses poison, erodes the remainder; and modest data-level adaptation (clean-source mixing, diffusion purification) closes what is left. The asymmetry is structural: forging a perturbation costs GPU-minutes per identity, while the funnel transformations that defeat it cost milliseconds to seconds per image on commodity platforms---and are chosen by parties with no stake in the attacker's success. Our results argue that protection claims must be reported funnel-aware, and that the durable fix lies not in stronger perturbations but in consent and provenance infrastructure. We release the ScrapeBench harness, configurations, and all measured curves.
