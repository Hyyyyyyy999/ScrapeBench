Face anti-customization methods are usually evaluated on clean files, although
images scraped from the Web may first be transcoded, rendered, cropped,
restored, regenerated, or purified. We introduce ScrapeBench, a paired benchmark
that applies each acquisition operation independently to the same source and
measures the retained protection gap against a same-identity clean control. The
revised protocol uses 100 CelebA-HQ identities (eight gallery and two held-out
reference images each), official GAP-Diff and IDProtector baselines, ARCP-v2,
two zero-shot readers, and 1,000-step DreamBooth customization. It fixes four
Web scenarios and reports FDFR, identity similarity, BRISQUE, and SER-FIQ with
identity-level uncertainty and equivalence tests. ARCP-v2 combines a true
pixel-space $16/255$ cap, band-limited perturbations, texture-aware allocation,
and acquisition-averaged face-reader objectives. The current revision freezes
the protocol before evaluation: numerical comparisons and superiority claims
will be populated only from the completed paired run.
