Protections that make personal photographs unlearnable by unauthorized diffusion-based customization are forged and validated in a laboratory---clean full-resolution files, lossless pipelines, a single tunable fine-tuning attacker. Images scraped from the web live in a different world. We name this distance the \emph{Web Acquisition Gap}: platform transcoding, browser and thumbnail rendering, agent-side capture, restoration, and diffusion-based regeneration standing between the file a defender protects and the copy an adversary trains on. \emph{ScrapeBench} is the first evaluation harness that treats this acquisition funnel as a first-class attack surface with per-tier attacker budgets, anchored in measured platform behavior (GitHub avatar re-encoding at JPEG quality 75, Gravatar at 90, WordPress Photon at 80). ScrapeBench reports the \emph{retention ratio} $\mathrm{RR}(T)$---the fraction of a defense's protection gap surviving tier $T$---against \emph{same-identity} clean baselines with paired significance tests, explicitly separating \emph{defense bypass} from \emph{image destruction}. Across six preprocessing operations, 40 public identities, two published defenses, two controls, a JPEG-aware retrain, and three attack families, the diagnosis is uncomfortable: under identity-matched baselines the headline gap shrinks roughly three-fold and loses statistical significance; no defense--operation pair retains a Holm-corrected gap at any signal-preserving tier; the JPEG-aware retrain buys nothing measurable; and the one operation lifting every retention ratio above one degrades the photographs themselves. What protection remains is reader-conditional. We complement the benchmark with \emph{ARCP}, an acquisition-robust camouflage perturbation that optimizes in expectation over the acquisition proxies and is, in a six-identity pilot, the only protection whose zero-shot gap survives JPEG transcoding and the canvas render ($\mathrm{RR}=.97$ and $1.18$) at an amplitude budget of $16/255$; and with \texttt{face-consent.txt}, a robots.txt-style consent protocol. We release the harness, platform measurements, defense protocols, and all curves.
