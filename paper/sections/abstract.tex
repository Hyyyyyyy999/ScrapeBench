Protections that promise to make personal photographs unlearnable by unauthorized diffusion-based customization are forged and validated in a laboratory: clean full-resolution files, lossless pipelines, and a single tunable fine-tuning attacker. Images scraped from the web live in a different world, and the distance between the two is the subject of this paper: the \emph{Web Acquisition Gap}---platform transcoding, browser and thumbnail rendering, agent-side capture, restoration, and diffusion-based regeneration standing between the file a defender protects and the copy an adversary actually trains on. We present \emph{ScrapeBench}, the first evaluation harness that treats this acquisition funnel as a first-class, tiered attack surface with per-tier attacker budgets, anchored in measured platform behavior (GitHub avatar re-encoding at JPEG quality 75, Gravatar at 90, WordPress Photon at 80). ScrapeBench reports a \emph{retention ratio} $\mathrm{RR}(T)$---the fraction of a defense's protection gap surviving at tier $T$---against \emph{same-identity} clean baselines, with paired significance tests and an explicit separation between \emph{defense bypass} and \emph{image destruction}. Across 40 public identities, four published defenses, two controls, a JPEG-aware variant, and three attack families, the diagnosis is uncomfortable: under the identity-matched baseline the headline T0 protection gap shrinks roughly three-fold and loses statistical significance; platform tiers erode the remainder within identity noise; and what protection exists is \emph{reader-conditional}---large against the fine-tuning adversary, small under IP-Adapter-FaceID, but measurable in a completed six-identity InstantID pilot. The funnel's most expensive tier manufactures apparent protection partly by destroying images, while a JPEG-aware retrain buys nothing measurable. Forging costs GPU-minutes per identity while defeating costs milliseconds per image. We complement the benchmark with \texttt{face-consent.txt}, a robots.txt-style consent protocol: durable protection is an acquisition-layer systems problem, not a stronger-perturbation problem. We release the harness, platform measurements, consent prototype, and all curves.
