On a Web increasingly read by generative agents, publishing a portrait and
authorizing a model of one's face are hard to keep apart: once an image is
uploaded, ordinary acquisition steps silently decide what a customization
agent actually receives. Existing anti-customization defenses are validated on
pristine files, although an agent may acquire a photograph after a single
ordinary operation such as CDN transcoding, feed rendering, detector cropping,
restoration, generative re-upload, or purification. We introduce
\emph{ScrapeBench}, an acquisition-contract benchmark that evaluates each such
operation independently from the same upload, with a matched clean branch,
held-out identity references, and an explicit clean-validity gate that
prevents image destruction from being credited as protection. The benchmark is
paired with a machine-readable consent-manifest direction for compliant agents;
it is not a propagation or enforcement mechanism.
We further propose ARCP-Web, a reference defense that allocates a bounded,
texture-aware perturbation across coarse and detail grids and concentrates its
optimization budget on the currently weakest acquisition condition. In a
preregistered four-identity feasibility pilot spanning two zero-shot identity
readers and a matrix of independent Web interventions, ARCP-Web has the largest
paired-gap point estimate in nine of 11 rankable reader--layer cells. This is a
falsification pilot, not a population or strict common-budget SOTA claim. We
release the harness, intervention registry, per-sample ledgers, a preregistered
40-identity expansion, and a \texttt{face-consent.txt} design direction for
future Web systems.
