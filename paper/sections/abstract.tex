Protections that promise to make personal photographs unlearnable by unauthorized diffusion-based customization are forged and validated in a laboratory: clean full-resolution files, lossless pipelines, and a single tunable fine-tuning attacker. Images scraped from the web live in a different world, and the distance between the two is the subject of this paper: the \emph{Web Acquisition Gap}---platform transcoding, browser and thumbnail rendering, agent-side capture, restoration, and diffusion-based regeneration standing between the file a defender protects and the copy an adversary actually trains on. We present \emph{ScrapeBench}, the first evaluation harness that treats this acquisition funnel as a first-class attack surface with per-tier attacker budgets, anchored in measured platform behavior (GitHub avatar re-encoding at JPEG quality 75, Gravatar at 90, WordPress Photon at 80). ScrapeBench reports a \emph{retention ratio} $\mathrm{RR}(T)$---the fraction of a defense's protection gap surviving preprocessing tier $T$---against \emph{same-identity} clean baselines, with paired significance tests and an explicit separation between \emph{defense bypass} and \emph{image destruction}. Evaluating each of six representative preprocessing operations in isolation across 40 public identities, two published defenses, two controls, a JPEG-aware retrain, and three attack families, the diagnosis is uncomfortable: under the identity-matched baseline the headline T0 protection gap shrinks roughly three-fold and loses statistical significance; no defense--operation pair retains a Holm-corrected gap at any signal-preserving tier (the regeneration tier's corrected gaps coincide with a collapsed clean baseline); the JPEG-aware retrain buys nothing measurable; and the one operation that lifts every retention ratio above one does so by degrading the photographs themselves. What protection exists is \emph{reader-conditional}---large against the fine-tuning adversary, small under IP-Adapter-FaceID, measurable in a completed six-identity InstantID pilot. Forging costs GPU-minutes per identity while defeating costs milliseconds per image. We complement the benchmark with \emph{ARCP}, an acquisition-robust camouflage perturbation that optimizes in expectation over the acquisition proxies with a face-metric reader-repulsion objective and a texture-allocated perceptual budget; in six-identity pilot evaluation it is the only protection whose zero-shot gap survives JPEG transcoding and the canvas render (retention ratios $.97$ and $1.18$) at an amplitude budget capped at $16/255$, and with \texttt{face-consent.txt}, a robots.txt-style consent protocol: durable protection is an acquisition-layer systems problem, not a stronger-perturbation problem. We release the harness, platform measurements, both defense protocols, the consent prototype, and all curves.
