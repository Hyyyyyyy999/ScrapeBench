Protections that promise to make personal photographs unlearnable by unauthorized diffusion-based customization are forged and validated in a laboratory: clean full-resolution files, lossless pipelines, and a single tunable fine-tuning attacker. Images scraped from the web live in a different world, and the distance between the two worlds is the subject of this paper. We name it the \emph{Web Acquisition Gap}: the stack of lossy transformations that lies between the file a defender protects and the copy an adversary actually trains on---platform transcoding, browser and thumbnail rendering, agent-side capture, restoration, and diffusion-based regeneration. We present \emph{ScrapeBench}, the first evaluation harness that treats this acquisition funnel as a first-class, tiered attack surface: seven tiers with explicit per-image attacker budgets, whose parameters are anchored not in speculation but in measured platform behavior (GitHub avatar re-encoding at JPEG quality 75, Gravatar at 90, WordPress Photon at 80, image proxies exposing the whole 60--90 band). ScrapeBench reports a \emph{retention ratio} $\mathrm{RR}(T)$---the fraction of a defense's clean-domain protection gap that survives at tier $T$---with paired significance tests, an integrated funnel protection score, and an explicit separation between \emph{defense bypass} and \emph{image destruction}. Across 40 public identities, four $T_0$-optimized perturbation defenses and two controls, three attack families (DreamBooth-LoRA, zero-shot IP-Adapter-FaceID, and InstantID), we find the gap is one-sided and structural: the platform's own tiers erase most of the T0 protection gap before any adaptive attacker acts; the order and composition of transformations do not rescue the defenses; a JPEG-aware retrained variant recovers part of the transcoding tier yet remains exposed to rendering and regeneration; and modest data-level adaptation closes what remains, while the cheapest tiers convert some defenses into image destruction rather than protection. Forging costs GPU-minutes per identity; defeating costs milliseconds per image, chosen by parties with no stake in the attack. We complement the benchmark with \texttt{face-consent.txt}, a robots.txt-style machine-readable consent protocol with a reference validator, and argue that durable protection is an acquisition-layer systems problem, not a stronger-perturbation problem. We release the harness, platform measurements, consent prototype, and all curves.
