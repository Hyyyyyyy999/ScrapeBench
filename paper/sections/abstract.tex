Protections that promise to make personal photographs unlearnable by unauthorized diffusion-based customization are forged and validated in a laboratory: clean full-resolution files, lossless pipelines, and a single tunable fine-tuning attacker. Images scraped from the web live in a different world, and the distance between the two worlds is the subject of this paper. We name it the \emph{Web Acquisition Gap}: the stack of lossy transformations that lies between the file a defender protects and the copy an adversary actually trains on---platform transcoding, browser and thumbnail rendering, agent-side capture, restoration, and diffusion-based regeneration. We present \emph{ScrapeBench}, the first evaluation harness that treats this acquisition funnel as a first-class, tiered attack surface with per-tier attacker budgets, anchored in measured platform behavior (GitHub avatar re-encoding at JPEG quality 75, Gravatar at 90, WordPress Photon at 80). ScrapeBench reports a \emph{retention ratio} $\mathrm{RR}(T)$---the fraction of a defense's protection gap that survives at tier $T$---against \emph{same-identity} clean baselines, with paired significance tests and an explicit separation between \emph{defense bypass} and \emph{image destruction}. Running it over 40 public identities against four published defenses, two controls, a JPEG-aware variant, and three attack families yields an uncomfortable diagnosis. Under the identity-matched baseline the field reports against, the headline T0 protection gap shrinks roughly three-fold and loses statistical significance; the funnel's platform tiers erode the remainder slowly, within identity noise; the protection that does exist is \emph{attack-conditional}---large against the fine-tuning adversary it was built for ($68\%$ reduction), negligible against zero-shot extractors that never traverse the poisoned gradient; the funnel's most expensive tier manufactures apparent protection by destroying images rather than preserving them, and no ordering of the funnel's transformations changes this picture; a JPEG-aware retrain buys nothing measurable. Forging still costs GPU-minutes per identity while defeating costs milliseconds per image. We complement the benchmark with \texttt{face-consent.txt}, a robots.txt-style machine-readable consent protocol with a reference validator, and argue that durable protection is an acquisition-layer systems problem, not a stronger-perturbation problem. We release the harness, platform measurements, consent prototype, and all curves.
