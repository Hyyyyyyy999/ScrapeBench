On a Web increasingly read by generative agents, publishing a portrait and
authorizing a model of one's face are hard to keep apart: once an image is
uploaded, ordinary acquisition steps silently decide what a customization
agent actually receives. Existing anti-customization defenses are validated on
pristine files, although an agent may acquire a photograph after a single
ordinary operation such as CDN transcoding, feed rendering, detector cropping,
restoration, generative re-upload, or purification. We introduce
\emph{ScrapeBench}, an acquisition-contract benchmark that evaluates each such
operation independently from the same upload, with a matched clean branch,
held-out identity references, and an explicit clean-validity gate that
prevents image destruction from being credited as protection. A preregistered
four-identity feasibility pilot---fixed before any defense outcome was
inspected---compares clean uploads against three released robustness-oriented
defenses (GAP-Diff, MetaCloak, GoodAC) and our reference defense ARCP-Web,
under two zero-shot readers (InstantID and IP-Adapter-FaceID). ARCP-Web
allocates its bounded perturbation across texture regions with a coarse-detail
design and concentrates optimization on the currently weakest acquisition
condition. Across the eleven rankable reader--layer conditions, ARCP-Web
achieves the largest paired protection gap in nine, with bootstrap intervals
excluding zero, at a strict $8/255$ pixel budget and 37.8\,dB mean PSNR;
the generative re-upload layer (T5) remains open for every defense, which is
precisely where consent metadata must carry what pixels cannot. The pilot
establishes effect direction and protocol integrity; the identical protocol
extends to a pre-audited 40-identity pool. We release the harness, intervention
registry, per-sample ledgers, and a machine-readable \texttt{face-consent.txt}
direction for carrying consent when pixel defenses fail.
