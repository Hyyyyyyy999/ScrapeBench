\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced ScrapeBench, an evaluation protocol that treats the web acquisition funnel as a first-class adversary of face anti-customization defenses. Across four published protections, a JPEG-aware retrain, and two controls, the headline gap shrinks roughly three-fold under identity-matched baselines and loses corrected significance (F1). What remains is reader-conditional rather than uniformly bypassed: IP-Adapter-FaceID shows a small matched gap, whereas a completed six-identity InstantID pilot shows a larger but underpowered one (F2/F4). Regeneration can additionally manufacture apparent protection by degrading face detectability (F3). Because funnel transformations cost orders of magnitude less than protection generation, pixel-space protection alone cannot provide a reader-independent deployment guarantee (F5). ScrapeBench's release---tier registry, replayable runners, detection-aware RR reporting, and repaired integrity pilots---aims to make collapse curves the field's currency and to redirect research toward consent provenance and enforceable platform obligations.
