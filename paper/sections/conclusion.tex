\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced ScrapeBench, an evaluation protocol that treats the web acquisition funnel---transcoding, browser and thumbnail rendering, agent capture, restoration, and diffusion-based regeneration---as the primary adversary of face anti-customization defenses. Measuring two published protections and two controls across two attack families under ten funnel tiers, we found that protections optimized for the laboratory's clean domain lose roughly half of their protection gap to a single JPEG-85 re-encode and nearly all of it by the browser tier, that zero-shot identity injection extracts comparable identity without touching the fine-tuning gradients that perturbation defenses poison, and that modest data-level adaptation---clean-source mixing and diffusion purification---closes the remaining gap. Because funnel transformations cost orders of magnitude less than protection generation and are selected by parties with no stake in the attacker's success, the arms race they anchor is structurally unwinnable. ScrapeBench's release---a tier registry, replayable runners, and an RR-based reporting standard---is designed to make collapse curves, not clean-domain numbers, the field's currency, and to redirect research from stronger noise toward consent provenance and enforceable platform obligations.
