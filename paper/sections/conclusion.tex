\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced ScrapeBench, an evaluation protocol that treats the web acquisition funnel as a first-class adversary of face anti-customization defenses, and evaluated it where the deployment question actually lives: each of six representative preprocessing operations applied in isolation, under identity-matched paired baselines. Across four published protections, a JPEG-aware retrain, and two controls, the headline gap shrinks roughly three-fold under identity-matched baselines and loses corrected significance (F1); no defense--operation pair retains a Holm-corrected gap at any tier, the JPEG-aware retrain is null, and the regeneration tier's apparent protection is image destruction (F2); what remains is reader-conditional rather than uniformly bypassed (F3). As the constructive counterpart, ARCP optimizes in expectation over the acquisition distribution with a texture-allocated budget and a reader-diverse objective, providing the reference defense the reporting standard calls for (F4)---while the cost ledger keeps the economic asymmetry in view (F5). ScrapeBench's release---tier registry, replayable runners, detection-aware RR reporting, repaired integrity pilots, and both defense protocols---aims to make per-operation retention curves the field's currency and to redirect research toward acquisition-aware design and consent provenance.
