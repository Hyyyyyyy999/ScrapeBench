\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
ScrapeBench recasts face anti-customization as an acquisition contract for the
agentic Web. Its seven layers are independent interventions from one common T0,
not a cumulative funnel. The preregistered four-identity feasibility pilot
compares recent released-code defenses and ARCP-Web under two zero-shot
customization readers, with hard validity gates and paired reporting, and the
identical protocol has now been executed on the full pre-audited 40-identity
pool. The expansion reorders the field: IDProtector holds the largest paired
gap in all 11 rankable reader--layer cells, ARCP-Web follows in ten and matches
IDProtector under a strict common $8/255$ budget in ten of eleven cells while
uploading 5.4 dB more imperceptible images, and GAP-Diff's protection
collapses under acquisition. The generative re-upload tier T5 and the
purification tier T6 remain open for every defense---exactly the boundary at
which consent metadata, not pixels, must carry the contract. This combination
of falsifiable pixel tests and durable Web consent metadata offers a concrete
path from laboratory evaluation to enforceable human agency on the agentic
Web.
