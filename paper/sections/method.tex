\subsection{Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}
We consider a subject $s$ whose face images $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{m}$ are publicly reachable on the web, and a defense $D$ that releases protected images $\tilde{x}_i = x_i + \delta_i$, $\|\delta_i\|_\infty \le \epsilon$, claiming that downstream customization of $\{\tilde{x}_i\}$ fails to reproduce $s$. The adversary $\mathcal{A}$ harvests images of $s$ through the funnel $\Phi$ (Section~\ref{sec:funnel}), then attempts identity reconstruction with a pipeline $P$---fine-tuning-based (\emph{DreamBooth-LoRA}~\cite{ruiz2022dreambooth}) or zero-shot (\emph{IP-Adapter-FaceID}~\cite{ye2023ipadapter}, \emph{InstantID}~\cite{wang2024instantid}). We assume a \emph{resource-bounded} adversary: $P$ runs on one consumer GPU (24\,GB) with 25--30 denoising steps and does not adversarially optimize against $D$---the funnel requires no knowledge of $D$. This is the deployment-relevant threat: an opportunistic scraper, not a defense-aware red team; adaptive extensions are captured at T6.

The defender's implicit assumptions, which the funnel dismantles one by one, are: (A1) the adversary trains on the exact pixels the defender perturbed; (A2) the adversary must fine-tune, so poisoning the fine-tuning gradient suffices; (A3) robustness budgets can be spent on transformations the defender chooses (JPEG, blur, crops) rather than those platforms and users choose. Each tier violates one of these assumptions \emph{upstream} of the defense (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).

\subsection{Funnel Formalization}
\label{sec:funnel}
The funnel is a sequence of transformations $\Phi = \phi_T \circ \cdots \circ \phi_1$ applied between upload and training-batch ingestion. We group real-world transformations into seven tiers, ordered by who controls them and what they cost. Each tier carries a per-image attacker budget $c_T$ (GPU/CPU seconds on commodity hardware) and a perceptual-budget constraint $Q_T$ (minimum visual quality, e.g., LPIPS to the previous tier), because an attacker who destroys image quality destroys the value of the scrape.

Concretely, T1 emulates platform transcoding with libjpeg-quality sweeps plus a composite \texttt{t1\_platform} (JPEG-85 at reduced long side, as served by major social platforms). T2 renders the image as a browser would: the image is rasterized into a viewport under device-pixel-ratio $\mathrm{DPR}\in\{1,2\}$ and re-captured, with a thumbnail variant at $240$\,px; where a headless browser is unavailable we substitute a deterministic canvas-equivalent rasterizer so that the tier is reproducible without browser drift. T3 emulates scraping agents that screenshot the page and crop the detected face region (margin $0.35$) rather than fetch the original file. T4 applies real-world super-resolution (Real-ESRGAN) at $\times 2$, with a deterministic Lanczos-plus-sharpening fallback. T5 applies diffusion-based regeneration: img2img at strengths $s\in\{0.3,0.5\}$ with an empty prompt and guidance $1.0$, and a DDIM-inversion-and-reconstruction path with $\eta=0.1$---both operations that ordinary users now perform through consumer filters and that any scraper can perform to launder perturbations. T6 models adaptive adversaries in two ways: \emph{mixing} (the attacker trains on $\rho$ fraction of clean images harvested from a second source, diluting the poisoned gallery exactly as in the uncontrolled setting of prior evaluations) and \emph{purification} (training on T5-regenerated versions of the protected images, laundering the perturbation before fine-tuning).

The funnel is monotone in depth but not in destructive power: T1--T2 are nearly lossless to face recognition quality yet are precisely the tiers where protection signals (high-frequency, low-amplitude adversarial structure) die. This is the crux: the funnel is a \emph{feature} of the attacker's environment, selected for free by platform economics.\sloppy

\subsection{Retention Ratio and Cost Ledger}
\label{sec:metric}
Let $g(x; P, r)$ denote the best cosine similarity between the ArcFace embedding~\cite{deng2018arcface} of a generated image $x$ and the reference embeddings of subject $s$ under attack pipeline $P$ with random seed $r$. For a defense $D$ and funnel tier $T$ we define the \emph{protection gap}
\begin{equation}
\Delta(T; D, P) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{r}\big[g(\Phi_T(\mathrm{clean}); P, r)\big] \;-\; \mathbb{E}_{r}\big[g(\Phi_T(D(\mathrm{clean})); P, r)\big],
\label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
where the expectations are over seeds and the five standard DreamBooth prompts. The first term is how well the attack reproduces $s$ from \emph{unprotected} images at the same tier---the fair baseline, because acquisition transforms hit attacker and defense alike. The \emph{retention ratio} normalizes the gap by its clean-domain value:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RR}(T; D, P) \;=\; \Delta(T; D, P)\,/\,\Delta(T_0; D, P).
\label{eq:rr}
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{RR}=1$ means the defense retains its full headline protection at tier $T$; $\mathrm{RR}\to 0$ means the funnel has erased it; negative values mean the funnel hurts the defense more than the attack. RR is robust to tier-level quality effects that confound raw-similarity comparisons, is comparable across defenses with different absolute strength, and yields a single interpretable curve per defense---the \emph{collapse curve} we report as the primary result.

Two companion quantities complete the ledger. \emph{Detection-failure rate}: the fraction of generated samples below the recognition threshold $\tau$ (calibrated on clean data, $\tau=0.30$ with sensitivity analysis at $0.25/0.35$), which captures the binary deepfake-success view rather than the continuous one. \emph{Infeasibility rate}: the fraction of cells in which the attack stack cannot operate at all because no face is detectable in the tiered gallery---the regime where the funnel has not merely bypassed a defense but destroyed the image's identity content. Keeping these two failure modes apart is essential for honest interpretation: a defense whose gallery survives T5 regeneration has not been \emph{bypassed} if the regeneration also destroyed the photograph's subject, and a platform that regenerates uploads aggressively protects nobody's face while harming every user. \emph{Cost asymmetry}: the GPU-seconds per identity to generate the protection ($c_{\mathrm{prot}}$) versus the CPU/GPU-seconds per image to execute the funnel transformations ($c_{\mathrm{funnel}}$); the ratio $\kappa = c_{\mathrm{prot}} / c_{\mathrm{funnel}}$ is the economic inversion that Section~\ref{sec:results} quantifies. We report 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals over identities, paired two-sided Wilcoxon tests against the clean baseline at matched tiers with Holm correction across tiers, and Cliff's $\delta$ effect sizes.

\subsection{Anchoring the Funnel in Measured Platform Behavior}
\label{sec:reality}
A tier definition is only as credible as its parameter range, so we probe four production image pipelines directly: GitHub's avatar service, Gravatar, WordPress's Photon CDN, and the open proxy \texttt{wsrv.nl}. For each response we estimate the JPEG quality factor by matching quantization tables against a reference encoding sweep (Table~\ref{tab:reality}). GitHub re-encodes JPEG avatars at quality ${\approx}75$ and clamps oversized requests to the stored resolution; Gravatar serves quality ${\approx}90$; Photon honors an explicit quality parameter (measured exact at 60/80/90); the proxy exposes the whole 60--90 band. Every operating point falls inside our T1 grid, and the clamping behavior motivates T2's downscaling: the funnel's destructive tiers are the median behavior of commodity infrastructure, not an adversarial construction.

\subsection{A Consent Layer That Survives the Funnel}
\label{sec:consent}
If perturbations do not survive acquisition but metadata does, the durable fix is to make consent ride with the image rather than inside it. We specify \texttt{face-consent.txt}, a robots.txt-style manifest at \texttt{/.well-known/face-consent.txt}: per-subject blocks with salted subject hashes, image globs, a use registry (\texttt{fine-tuning} and \texttt{embedding-extraction} are first-class tokens mirroring our two attack families), expiry, revocation endpoints, optional detached signatures, and a default-deny rule for unmatched paths and unlisted uses. A reference validator shipped with the harness (under 200 lines) validates manifests, issues per-URL allow/deny decisions citing the deciding rule, emits a manifest hash so a scraper can prove which consent it acted on, and audits directories for coverage. The protocol needs nothing beyond static file serving; it is the constructive complement to our negative results---perturbations fight the funnel and lose, consent travels with the funnel and survives.
