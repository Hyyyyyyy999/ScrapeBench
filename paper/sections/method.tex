\section{The ScrapeBench Acquisition Contract}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Threat model}
Let subject $s$ publish gallery $X_s$ and defense $D$ release
$\widetilde{X}_s=D(X_s)$. An agent obtains either branch through exactly one
acquisition intervention $\phi_k$ and passes it to customization pipeline $P$.
The held-out reference set $R_s$ is never protected, transformed, or supplied to
$P$; it is used only for evaluation. The benchmark pairs
$P(\phi_k(X_s))$ with $P(\phi_k(\widetilde{X}_s))$ using the same prompt and
seed. Thus a platform operation cannot be mistaken for protection.

\subsection{Seven independent interventions}
\label{sec:interventions}
The intervention set $\Phi=\{\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_6\}$ is a flat matrix, not a
funnel: each $\phi_k$ consumes T0 and never the output of another row.

T1 matches GAP-Diff's primary JPEG setting and sits at the pessimistic end of
the CDN qualities we measured (GitHub$\approx$75, WordPress$\approx$80). T2
approximates a common feed card; T3 represents an agent that sees rendered
pixels rather than the origin file; T4--T6 test increasingly model-mediated
acquisition. T6 is explicitly a
prompt-free reconstruction proxy, not an implementation claimed to be the
official DiffPure algorithm. Each layer has a usability check on its input face.

\subsection{Metrics and validity gates}
Let $e(y,R_s)$ be the maximum ArcFace cosine similarity between generated image
$y$ and valid references of $s$. We report conditional identity similarity
(ISM) over detected generated faces and end-to-end ISM, where detection failures
receive zero. Face-detection failure rate (FDFR) is reported separately, so an
undetectable image is never silently credited as identity-specific protection.
For defense $D$, attack $P$, and layer $k$, the paired protection gap is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{k,D,P}=\mathbb{E}_s[\mathrm{ISM}_{k,\mathrm{clean},P}(s)-
\mathrm{ISM}_{k,D,P}(s)].
\end{equation}
Higher $\Delta$ is better. Retention ratio is
$\mathrm{RR}_{k,D,P}=\Delta_{k,D,P}/\Delta_{0,D,P}$, accompanied by a paired
identity-bootstrap interval and the absolute change $\Delta_k-\Delta_0$.
Protected uploads are audited by their realized per-image $L_\infty$ after
8-bit quantization and by PSNR. A layer is
rankable only when the matched clean attack detects at least 50\% of outputs
and retains at least 50\% of its clean-T0 end-to-end ISM. A defense is called
effective only when that gate passes and the 95\% interval for $\Delta$
excludes zero.

\subsection{Beyond pixels: consent carried by the Web}
No bounded perturbation can guarantee survival under arbitrary reconstruction.
We therefore sketch \path{/.well-known/face-consent.txt}, a machine-readable
manifest mapping image patterns and subject hashes to permitted uses, expiry,
revocation, and optional signatures. In its present form this is only a
voluntary preference and audit hook for compliant agents. It neither proves
that an uploader can authorize every depicted subject, locates a source after
arbitrary copying, nor constrains a malicious agent. ScrapeBench supplies the
empirical test for pixel defenses; origin binding, subject verification,
cross-domain propagation, conflict resolution, and enforcement remain separate
Web-systems research problems.
