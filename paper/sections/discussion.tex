\section{Discussion: What Survives the Funnel}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Perturbation-Based Protection Is Structurally Fragile}
The collapse we measure is not a collection of implementation artifacts; it follows from what protective perturbations are. A perturbation is a low-amplitude, typically high-frequency signal tuned to a specific computational path---the gradient of a fine-tuning loss through a particular denoiser. The funnel's platform tiers were never designed to preserve such signals: transcoders optimize rate--distortion, browsers resample for display, agents crop for relevance; each operation is benign for semantic content and lethal for the perturbation's supporting structure, and their composition multiplies. The zero-shot attack family removes the very surface the perturbation poisons: IP-Adapter-FaceID and InstantID read an identity embedding off the funnel output and inject it through cross-attention, so there is no fine-tuning gradient to corrupt. A defense family whose mechanism is gradient poisoning does not, as currently formulated, model the pipeline it is deployed behind---and so cannot certify itself against it. Recent limits-focused analyses reach consonant conclusions from the laboratory side~\cite{chen2025fragile}; our contribution is to show that no laboratory attacker is needed---the commodity web is adversary enough.

\subsection{A Reporting Standard: RR@T}
We propose that protection papers report, alongside T0 numbers: (i) the collapse curve RR$(T)$ over the public funnel tiers, (ii) the tier at which the Holm-corrected gap first becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero, and (iii) the cost ledger $(c_{\mathrm{prot}}, c_{\mathrm{funnel}}, \kappa)$. This costs a page and changes the field's incentives: a defense that is strong only at T0 can no longer headline a single number. The tiers are intentionally implementable with commodity libraries so that adoption is a scripting exercise, not a research project; our release ships the registry.

\subsection{The Durable Fix: Consent Provenance, Not Stronger Noise}
If F1--F4 hold, the marginal return on stronger perturbations is small: the funnel is free and adaptive tiers launder what survives (Section~\ref{sec:consent} specifies the manifest; here we draw its implications). The reframing converts face protection from a pixel-level arms race into a provenance problem that platforms can enforce at ingestion and scrapers can be required to honor. Empirical study of web-scale consent mechanisms shows that data owners today have no effective way to say no~\cite{lee2025data}; a standardized manifest, compatible with emerging content-credential infrastructure~\cite{c2pa2026spec}, is the missing instrument. Three advantages follow: it survives the funnel by construction (consent is metadata, not pixels); it composes with content credentials rather than fighting them; and it gives benign ecosystem actors---platforms, model trainers, inference providers---a technically checkable obligation, which perturbations never provide. ScrapeBench's role here is motivational: by quantifying exactly how little of today's pixel-level protection survives acquisition, the funnel makes the case for structural remedies concrete.

\subsection{Limitations}
Our galleries use aligned celebrity corpora rather than in-the-wild profiles; aligned crops may slightly flatter both attacks and defenses. The funnel tiers are faithful reconstructions, not captured traces from specific platforms; platform-internal pipelines vary and may be harsher or gentler than our composites. We test four representative defenses from the open literature plus one JPEG-aware variant and two zero-shot attackers (IP-Adapter-FaceID and InstantID~\cite{wang2024instantid}); other published defenses---particularly the 2026 injection-hardening and perceptual-degradation designs---may behave differently, and evaluating them under the funnel is a natural continuation. Finally, ScrapeBench's adversary is resource-bounded and non-adaptive by design; a defense-aware red team with platform-grade infrastructure sits outside our threat model, and our bounds should be read as lower bounds on attacker capability.
