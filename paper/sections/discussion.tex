\section{Discussion: What Survives the Funnel}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why the Reported Protection Does Not Survive Contact}
Our diagnosis locates the primary failure in the evaluation protocol itself: a protection gap is a \emph{difference} between protected and clean conditions, and when the two are instantiated on different identities the difference absorbs identity-difficulty variance, which our measurements place at the same order as the effect being claimed. What remains after the correction is too small to survive single-tier preprocessing with statistical dignity: across the six representative operations, no defense--tier pair retains a Holm-corrected gap, retention ratios scatter within identity noise across neighboring JPEG qualities, and the JPEG-aware retrain demonstrates at matched budget that the failure is not a missing augmentation. Mechanism alone is also insufficient: embedding-injection readers remove the poisoned fine-tuning path, yet IP-Adapter-FaceID and InstantID respond differently to the same protected galleries. This explains the sharper reader-conditionality we measure (F3): protection under one training or conditioning path does not predict another. The funnel's expensive regeneration tier manufactures \emph{pseudo-protection} by degrading face detectability---which our conditional, detection, and unconditional views are designed to separate---and ARCP's design (F4) starts from exactly these measured failure modes: band-limitation against the transcode tiers, texture-allocated amplitude against the visibility ceiling, expectation over the acquisition proxies, and a reader term for the conditioning path that T0-only objectives never touch.

\subsection{A Reporting Standard: RR@T}
We propose that protection papers report, alongside T0 numbers: (i) the collapse curve RR$(T)$ over the public funnel tiers, computed against \emph{same-identity} clean controls, (ii) the tier at which the Holm-corrected gap first becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero, and (iii) the cost ledger $(c_{\mathrm{prot}}, c_{\mathrm{funnel}}, \kappa)$. This costs a page and changes the field's incentives: a defense strong only at T0 can no longer headline a single number, and one whose T0 number depends on an unmatched baseline can no longer borrow significance from identity variance.

\subsection{The Durable Fix: Consent Provenance, Not Stronger Noise}
If F1--F5 hold, the marginal return on stronger perturbations is bounded but not zero: ARCP is the strongest pixel-level response the measured failure modes suggest, and even it targets retention of a marginal gap rather than a guarantee. The measurement pipeline is cheap, switching readers changes the observed gap, and adaptive tiers can launder what survives (Section~\ref{sec:consent} specifies the manifest). The reframing converts face protection from a pixel-level arms race into a provenance problem that platforms can enforce at ingestion and scrapers can be required to honor. Empirical study of web-scale consent mechanisms shows that data owners today have no effective way to say no~\cite{lee2025data}; a standardized manifest, compatible with emerging content-credential infrastructure~\cite{c2pa2026spec}, is the missing instrument. Three advantages follow: it survives the funnel by construction (consent is metadata, not pixels); it composes with content credentials rather than fighting them; and it gives platforms, model trainers, and inference providers a technically checkable obligation, which perturbations never provide.

\subsection{Limitations}
Our galleries use aligned celebrity corpora rather than in-the-wild profiles; aligned crops may slightly flatter both attacks and defenses. The funnel tiers are faithful reconstructions, not captured platform traces; platform-internal pipelines may be harsher or gentler than our composites. We test four representative defenses, one JPEG-aware variant, and two zero-shot attackers (IP-Adapter-FaceID and InstantID~\cite{wang2024instantid}); the 2026 injection-hardening and perceptual-degradation designs may behave differently, and evaluating them under the funnel is a natural continuation. The repaired T3, InstantID, and Stage-C grids contain only six identities and ten generations per cell; none of the InstantID protected--clean contrasts survives correction across eight comparisons. Stage B remains cross-identity and directional, while Stage C lacks matched mix0/mix100 endpoints and therefore cannot identify the causal benefit of mixing or purification. ARCP's reader term is optimized against a differentiable VGGFace2 face-metric network while evaluation uses held-out readers, and the 20-identity IP-Adapter-FaceID grid shows the gap does not transfer to that reader (turning harmful at $\lambda=2$), so ARCP's scope is explicitly reader-conditional rather than general; its texture-allocated budget of up to $16/255$ in textured regions is argued from contrast masking and audited by PSNR/LPIPS accounting rather than a human study, which is the natural next step. Detection-dependent scoring is another source of uncertainty, which is why we report conditional similarity, detection rate, and a detection-failure-zero view separately. Larger identity pools and multiple generation seeds are needed to resolve whether the residual reader-specific gaps generalize. Finally, ScrapeBench's adversary is resource-bounded and non-adaptive by design; our bounds are lower bounds on attacker capability.
