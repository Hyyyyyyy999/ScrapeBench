\section{Discussion: What Survives the Funnel}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Perturbation-Based Protection Is Structurally Fragile}
The collapse we measure is not a collection of implementation artifacts; it follows from what protective perturbations are. A perturbation is a low-amplitude, typically high-frequency signal tuned to a specific computational path---the gradient of a fine-tuning loss through a particular denoiser. The funnel's platform tiers were never designed to preserve such signals: transcoders optimize rate--distortion, browsers resample for display, agents crop for relevance. Each operation is individually benign for semantic content and individually lethal for the perturbation's supporting structure, and their composition multiplies. Meanwhile the zero-shot attack family removes the very surface the perturbation poisons: IP-Adapter-FaceID reads an identity embedding off the funnel output and injects it through cross-attention, so there is no fine-tuning gradient to corrupt. A defense family whose mechanism is gradient poisoning and whose robustness story is adversarial training over hand-picked transformations cannot, in principle, certify itself against a pipeline it does not control. Recent limits-focused analyses reach consonant conclusions from the laboratory side~\cite{chen2025fragile}; our contribution is to show that no laboratory attacker is needed---the commodity web is adversary enough.

\subsection{A Reporting Standard: RR@T}
We propose that protection papers report, alongside T0 numbers: (i) the collapse curve RR$(T)$ over the public funnel tiers, (ii) the tier at which the Holm-corrected gap first becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero, and (iii) the cost ledger $(c_{\mathrm{prot}}, c_{\mathrm{funnel}}, \kappa)$. This costs a page and changes the field's incentives: a defense that is strong only at T0 can no longer headline a single number. The tiers are intentionally implementable with commodity libraries so that adoption is a scripting exercise, not a research project; our release ships the registry.

\subsection{The Durable Fix: Consent Provenance, Not Stronger Noise}
If F1--F3 hold, the marginal return on stronger perturbations is small: the funnel is free and adaptive tiers launder what survives. The alternative is to change what is being protected. A machine-readable consent manifest---\texttt{face-consent.txt} served beside the image, declaring identity, scope (train/generate/none), and revocation---converts face protection from a pixel-level arms race into a provenance problem that platforms can enforce at ingestion and scrapers can be required to honor. This reframing has three advantages: it survives the funnel by construction (consent is metadata, not pixels); it composes with emerging content-credential infrastructure rather than fighting it; and it gives benign ecosystem actors---platforms, model trainers, inference providers---a technically checkable obligation, which perturbations never provide. We view ScrapeBench's role here as motivational: by quantifying exactly how little of today's pixel-level protection survives acquisition, the funnel makes the case for structural remedies concrete.

\subsection{Limitations}
Our galleries use aligned celebrity corpora rather than in-the-wild profiles; aligned crops may slightly flatter both attacks and defenses. The funnel tiers are faithful reconstructions, not captured traces from specific platforms; platform-internal pipelines vary and may be harsher or gentler than our composites. We test representative defenses from the open literature rather than every published variant, and our zero-shot attacker uses one adapter family; other identity adapters (e.g., InstantID-class~\cite{wang2024instantid}) may interact differently with T4--T5. Finally, ScrapeBench's adversary is resource-bounded and non-adaptive by design; a defense-aware red team with platform-grade infrastructure sits outside our threat model, and our bounds should be read as lower bounds on attacker capability.
