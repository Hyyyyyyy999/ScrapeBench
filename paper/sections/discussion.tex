\section{Discussion: What Survives the Funnel}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why the Reported Protection Does Not Survive Contact}
Our diagnosis locates the primary failure upstream of the funnel, in the evaluation protocol itself. A protection gap is a \emph{difference} between a protected condition and a clean condition; when the two conditions are instantiated on different identities, the difference absorbs the identity-difficulty variance of the corpus, which our measurements place at the same order as the effect being claimed. The same hazard operates in reverse for mechanism: a perturbation is a low-amplitude signal tuned to one computational path---the gradient of a fine-tuning loss through a particular denoiser---and the zero-shot attack family removes that path entirely, reading an identity embedding off the funnel output and injecting it through cross-attention, so there is no fine-tuning gradient to corrupt. This explains the sharp attack-conditionality we measure (F2): the defense is real where it was designed to act and absent where deployment acts. The funnel's role is then not a cliff but a slow erosion---platform tiers damage the perturbation's supporting structure mildly and within identity noise (F1)---and its expensive regeneration tier manufactures \emph{pseudo-protection} by degrading every photograph, which our bypass-versus-destruction separation is designed to catch. Limits-focused analyses reach consonant conclusions from the laboratory side~\cite{chen2025fragile}; we show the commodity measurement pipeline is adversary enough.

\subsection{A Reporting Standard: RR@T}
We propose that protection papers report, alongside T0 numbers: (i) the collapse curve RR$(T)$ over the public funnel tiers, computed against \emph{same-identity} clean controls, (ii) the tier at which the Holm-corrected gap first becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero, and (iii) the cost ledger $(c_{\mathrm{prot}}, c_{\mathrm{funnel}}, \kappa)$. This costs a page and changes the field's incentives twice: a defense that is strong only at T0 can no longer headline a single number, and a defense whose T0 number depends on an unmatched baseline can no longer borrow significance from identity variance. The tiers are intentionally implementable with commodity libraries so that adoption is a scripting exercise, not a research project; our release ships the registry.

\subsection{The Durable Fix: Consent Provenance, Not Stronger Noise}
If F1--F4 hold, the marginal return on stronger perturbations is small: the measurement pipeline is free, zero-shot readers bypass the mechanism, and adaptive tiers launder what survives (Section~\ref{sec:consent} specifies the manifest; here we draw its implications). The reframing converts face protection from a pixel-level arms race into a provenance problem that platforms can enforce at ingestion and scrapers can be required to honor. Empirical study of web-scale consent mechanisms shows that data owners today have no effective way to say no~\cite{lee2025data}; a standardized manifest, compatible with emerging content-credential infrastructure~\cite{c2pa2026spec}, is the missing instrument. Three advantages follow: it survives the funnel by construction (consent is metadata, not pixels); it composes with content credentials rather than fighting them; and it gives benign ecosystem actors---platforms, model trainers, inference providers---a technically checkable obligation, which perturbations never provide. ScrapeBench's role here is motivational: by quantifying exactly how little of today's pixel-level protection survives acquisition, the funnel makes the case for structural remedies concrete.

\subsection{Limitations}
Our galleries use aligned celebrity corpora rather than in-the-wild profiles; aligned crops may slightly flatter both attacks and defenses. The funnel tiers are faithful reconstructions, not captured traces from specific platforms; platform-internal pipelines vary and may be harsher or gentler than our composites. We test four representative defenses from the open literature plus one JPEG-aware variant and two zero-shot attackers (IP-Adapter-FaceID and InstantID~\cite{wang2024instantid}); other published defenses---particularly the 2026 injection-hardening and perceptual-degradation designs---may behave differently, and evaluating them under the funnel is a natural continuation. Three planned components were excluded after integrity checks (Appendix~\ref{app:integrity}): the face-crop tier, the full-scale adaptation study, and all but one identity of the InstantID grid, whose single paired trajectory suggests---but cannot establish---that robustness does not transfer across zero-shot readers. Paired same-identity evaluation halves the effective sample size, and our per-tier gaps sit within one standard error of identity variance; larger identity pools could resolve whether the residual gap is truly zero or merely small. Finally, ScrapeBench's adversary is resource-bounded and non-adaptive by design; a defense-aware red team with platform-grade infrastructure sits outside our threat model, and our bounds should be read as lower bounds on attacker capability.
